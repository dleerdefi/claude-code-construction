## 3. Best (b) items

1. **if.08-door-airflow** — "Need from them: Doors the air design relies on for transfer or return air, room pressure relationships, and stair and smoke-zone pressure differences that set door opening and closing forces … If missed: The air design returns air through door undercuts, but the doors are gasketed smoke or acoustic doors with seals and bottoms." The baseline has no HVAC interface at all; a hardware reviewer would approve gasketing and door bottoms on doors the TAB design needs as return-air paths, and size stair closers without the pressurization differential.

2. **d08.rc.rated-openings** — "Every opening in a rated or smoke-resistant wall on the life safety plans has an opening protective in the door schedule with the rating that wall needs … Ratings copied from an earlier life safety plan, and walls upgraded after the schedule was written, show up here." The baseline (#11, #31) checks hardware index against door-schedule rating and stops; it inherits a wrong schedule rating and approves a non-rated set on a door in a rated wall.

3. **edu.storm-shelter** — "every element of the shelter envelope (… doors with their frames and hardware …) is submitted with evidence that it meets ICC 500 pressure and debris-impact testing as the tested assembly … One standard door, louver or hardware substitution in the shelter boundary breaks the shelter." Absent from baseline; on a K-12 in tornado country the hardware sets for shelter doors must be the tested assembly's hardware, and a BOD-equivalent swap would pass the baseline review.

4. **edu.agency-approvals** — "Where a state school-construction agency has jurisdiction, deferred submittals, substitutions and field changes that touch structure, fire and life safety or accessibility are approved by that agency before fabrication, not only by the architect." Baseline routes only to AHJ/fire marshal/district; a hardware substitution on rated or accessible doors approved by the architect alone is a certification hold on DSA-type projects.

5. **if.03-floor-recesses** — "Blockouts and recesses formed at the pour to the approved product (floor closer and pivot boxes, sliding-door floor tracks and recessed thresholds …)". Baseline #30 settles threshold elevations before the pour but never flags floor closers/pivots at aluminum entrances, which are selected in the hardware sets and need a recess the concrete crew never hears about.

## 4. Every (c) item

1. **08hw.accessible-operation** — "Operable hardware on accessible doors works without tight grasping or twisting and sits within the reach range; closers can be set to the opening force and closing speed the hook requires; thresholds stay within the height and bevel limits; and where the force cannot be met (exterior doors, pressurized stairs, heavy doors) a power operator is in the set." The first three clauses match baseline #43–45. The last clause is wrong as a conformance check: the 2010 ADA Standards (404.2.9) and ICC A117.1 exempt exterior doors and fire doors from the interior opening-force limit and do not require a power operator; whether an operator is required is a local-code/owner-standard question (which the paired hook 08hw.rh.accessible-operation correctly asks). As written a reviewer would reject exterior and stair sets lacking operators.

2. **ANSI/BHMA A156.28: Recommended Practices for Mechanical Keying Systems** — the standard's title is "Recommended Practices for Keying Systems"; the inserted word "Mechanical" mis-titles it. Minor, but the protocol counts a mis-titled standard as (c). Baseline #65 cites it correctly.

## 5. Reverse gaps (baseline items COMPILED lacks entirely)

1. Hinge count, size and weight rules; NRP on outswinging exterior/secured doors; electric hinge wire count vs device (baseline #14). Compiled never checks hinges at all, and omits A156.1/A156.7/A156.26.
2. Lock function by room use against floor plans — classroom security, storeroom on MEP/IT rooms, privacy with indicator on single toilets, passage (baseline #13). Compiled checks lockdown function only.
3. Closer arm type vs swing and wall condition; backcheck/delayed action on exterior; pull-side only where parallel arm impossible (baseline #15).
4. Exit device type (rim/SVR/CVR/mortise) vs door type and pairs/mullions; less-bottom-rod only with listed fire pin (baseline #16).
5. Handing convention mismatch between architect schedule and supplier (baseline #11, #51) — one of the most common reorders, absent from compiled checks and failure modes.
6. Finish consistency and corrosion-resistant finishes at exterior/wet areas (baseline #17); exterior/acoustic gasketing, sweeps, rain drips, astragals and threshold profile vs floor transition (baseline #18); A156.21/A156.22 standards.
7. Accessories: silencers, kick/armor plate sizing, wall/floor/overhead stops at every swing, in-wall blocking for stops (baseline #19, #28), and overhead stop vs closer interference (baseline #60).
8. Template numbers and prep information per item so HM and wood fabricators can prep (baseline #6, #32) — compiled routing says "templates" but never lists them as a required submittal content.
9. Warranty statement per category and installer qualification for electrified products and operators (baseline #8).
10. Voltage drop on remote 24 VDC power supplies (baseline #54); mixed-manufacturer templating in one opening (baseline #57); hardware packaged by set and door number (baseline #63); keying conference timed 8–10 weeks before substantial completion so permanent cores arrive (baseline #29). Also missing from standards: UL 10C/10B, A156.6, A156.8, A156.21, A156.22, SDI A250.8, DHI schedule/keying formats, ICC A117.1.

## 6. Verdict

(a)+(d) dominate at 92% of compiled items: the compiled layer restates the expert baseline on egress, rated-opening function, keying, electrified/fire-alarm reconciliation and the ANSI/BHMA standards, and pads it with global Division 01 boilerplate, K-12 duplicates (storm shelter and agency review each appear three times), and two misrouted glazing/energy items. The five genuine (b) items (HVAC door-airflow interface, life-safety-plan rating drift, ICC 500 shelter hardware, state school-agency approval, floor-closer recesses) are real but narrow, while the layer entirely misses the opening-by-opening product checks (hinges, lock functions, closer arms, exit device types, handing, finishes, accessories, templates) that make up most of an actual hardware review.
