## 3. Best (b) items

1. "**edu.agency-approvals** — Where a state school-construction agency has jurisdiction, deferred submittals, substitutions and field changes that touch structure, fire and life safety or accessibility are approved by that agency before fabrication, not only by the architect..." — On a DSA/TDLR school, an RTU substitution changes weight, anchorage and curb; the architect's stamp does not make it legal to fabricate. Baseline only asks the agency about ventilation/acoustic rules.
2. "**d23.rc.intake-separation** — The unit's outdoor air intake keeps the required separation from every exhaust or relief outlet, plumbing vent, flue, cooling tower and vehicle area on any discipline's roof plan or elevations..." — Forces a look at the plumbing roof plan and the kitchen hood exhaust, which the baseline's gas-flue-only check (64) does not.
3. "**23ah.rh.energy-recovery** — Does the adopted energy code require exhaust air energy recovery for this unit's outdoor air fraction, airflow, operating hours and climate zone, and what effectiveness must it reach?" — High-OA DCV classroom RTUs are exactly the units that cross the 90.1 6.5.6.1 threshold; the baseline treats ERV as optional equipment to rate, not a possible code requirement.
4. "**23ah.rh.refrigerant-gwp** — Which refrigerants may the packaged and split DX units on this project use under federal and state high-GWP restrictions, given each unit's manufacture and installation dates?" — The install-date question is separate from "does the AHJ accept A2L"; a legacy R-410A unit approved to match an old spec can become uninstallable.
5. "**if.01-permanent-hvac-construction-use** — Agree warranty start and filtration before the equipment is started for construction use..." — RTUs run for drywall drying with no return filtration and warranty clocks started are a routine K-12 loss the baseline never raises.

## 4. Every (c) item

- "**AHRI 1350: Mechanical Performance Rating of Central Station Air-handling Unit Casings**" — Correctly titled but misapplied to 23 74 13. AHRI 1350 rates casing deflection/leakage of central-station (custom/semi-custom) AHUs; packaged rooftop units are not certified to it, so listing it as a standard to verify RTU claims against sends the reviewer asking for a rating the manufacturer cannot provide. Inherited from the 23 70 00 ancestor without a section filter.

## 6. Verdict

Yes, (a)+(d) dominate at 94%: the compiled context is a competent restatement of what an expert PE lists closed-book, padded with ancestor-inherited items (louvers, foodservice, housekeeping pads, concealed access, drive heat) that do not apply to a rooftop unit, and it omits the RTU-specific depth the baseline carries (sensible capacity, SCCR, gas heat, heat-pump data, controls ownership, rigging, sound standards). The five (b) items — state-agency approval of substitutions, intake separation across disciplines, the ERV code trigger, the GWP install-date question and construction-use warranty — are real and worth keeping, but a dedicated 23 74 13 profile is needed before this layer adds more than it costs.
