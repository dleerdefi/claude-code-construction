# 08 71 00 Door Hardware — Submittal Review Checklist (GC Project Engineer baseline)

Assumed project: commercial office or K-12 school. Healthcare (I-2) differences are called out where they change the review.

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Hardware schedule in vertical (set-by-set) format: each set lists quantity, item type, manufacturer, catalog number with all option suffixes, ANSI/BHMA function number, BHMA finish code, fastener type, and handing; stamped by an AHC (Architectural Hardware Consultant).
2. Door-and-hardware index: one row per door number from the architect's door schedule, giving hardware set number, hand, door/frame material, fire rating, size, and whether the opening is electrified; every scheduled door appears exactly once, and the submittal explicitly lists openings excluded (storefront, auto doors, overhead doors) and who furnishes them.
3. Manufacturer catalog cuts for every line item, with the exact model, function, finish, lever/trim design, strike package, and option codes marked; an unmarked multi-option catalog page is not product data.
4. Keying schedule or keying-conference plan: keyway, key system structure (GMK/MK/SKD/KA/KD), cylinder type (SFIC vs. LFIC vs. conventional), construction keying method (temporary cores vs. construction master key), keys per cylinder, key control cabinet/software, and who installs permanent cores.
5. Electrified hardware package: point-to-point wiring diagram per opening type, a riser showing power supply → door, a power supply schedule (input voltage/circuit, output voltage, amps per output, battery backup, fire-alarm release input), and a door elevation locating the power transfer (EPT/electric hinge), junction box side, and raceway.
6. Template numbers and prep information for every item requiring door or frame prep (hinge size/location, strike type, closer reinforcement, exit device type, EPT cutout, concealed vertical rod prep) so hollow metal and wood door fabricators can prep.
7. Listing documentation for every item on a fire-rated or smoke door: UL/Intertek listing showing the exact product on the exact door type and rating; exit devices on rated doors shown as "Fire Exit Hardware" (not panic) with no mechanical dogging.
8. Warranty statement per product category (closers typically 10–30 yr, exit devices 3–5 yr, locks 3 yr, electrified 1–2 yr) and installer qualification statement (factory training for electrified products and auto operators).
9. Substitution status: if any manufacturer differs from the Basis of Design, an approved substitution request (or concurrent request with side-by-side function/grade/finish/listing comparison) must be in the package; otherwise the schedule is reviewed as a deviation.

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

10. Compare each submitted hardware set line-by-line against the specified set in 08 71 00 Part 3 (Hardware Sets); every added, deleted, or changed item, function, grade, or finish is cloud-marked.
11. Compare the index against the architect's door schedule (A-600 series) for door number, hand (using the handing convention stated in the spec/schedule — secure side vs. push side), rating, door/frame material, thickness, and size; any mismatch is a hold on that opening.
12. Verify ANSI/BHMA grades match the spec (Grade 1 locks, closers, and exit devices on office/school; heavy-weight Grade 1 hinges on doors over 36" wide, over 1-3/4" thick, or with closers in high-traffic corridors).
13. Verify lock function by room use against the floor plans: classroom security (F110) on classrooms; storeroom (F86) on mechanical/electrical/IT/janitor; office (F82) or entry (F109) on offices; privacy with occupancy indicator (F76/F19) on single-occupant toilets; passage (F75) on non-lockable rooms.
14. Check hinge count and size: 2 hinges up to 60" high plus one per additional 30"; 4-1/2 x 4-1/2 standard weight, 5 x 4-1/2 heavy weight where required; NRP (non-removable pin) on outswinging exterior and secured doors; electric hinge or EPT with wire count matching the electrified device.
15. Check closer selection against door location: arm type (regular, parallel, cush, hold-open) matches swing and wall condition on the plans; exterior closers are adjustable spring size with backcheck and delayed action where specified; closers on pull side of corridor doors only where a parallel arm is impossible.
16. Check exit devices: rim vs. surface vertical rod vs. concealed vertical rod vs. mortise matches the door type (pairs with/without mullion, glass doors), lever trim function matches set, less-bottom-rod on CVR pairs only with a listed fire pin, cylinder dogging on non-rated only.
17. Check finish consistency within each set and across the building (e.g., 626/630 satin chrome/stainless), and exterior or wet-area items in 630 stainless or other corrosion-resistant finish rather than plated brass-base.
18. Check exterior and acoustic openings for threshold, gasketing, sweep, rain drip, and astragal; threshold height ≤ 1/2" and the threshold profile matches the floor transition shown on finish plans and wall sections.
19. Check accessories: silencers (3 per single frame, 2 per pair), kick/armor plates sized width minus 2" (push side) and 1" on pairs, wall stops or floor stops at every swing, overhead stops where no wall stop is possible, mounting-height notes for levers and pulls.
20. Reconcile electrified openings against the security/low-voltage drawings (T- or SC-series access control device schedule): every card-reader door has a locking device, power transfer, REX method, door position switch, and a named power supply; no electrified opening exists in one document only.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

21. Hollow metal doors and frames (08 11 13): send the approved hardware schedule and templates; receive frame and door prep shop drawings showing hinge reinforcement, strike prep, closer reinforcement, EPT cutout, and frame raceway; must be settled before frame fabrication release, which precedes stud layout since frames are set early.
22. Wood doors (08 14 16): send hardware schedule and templates; receive door prep drawings (lock/exit device prep, CVR prep, electrified raceway, vision lite coordination with exit device rail height); settled before door fabrication release.
23. Aluminum entrances/storefront (08 41 13) and automatic doors (08 42 29): confirm which trade furnishes and installs the hardware on each aluminum opening; send sets for those doors; receive entrance shop drawings showing rail sizes for exit device/closer and pivot/EPT prep; settled at storefront shop drawing approval.
24. Automatic door operators (08 71 13): send operator type (low-energy vs. full-power), actuator locations, and sequencing with the electric strike/latch retraction; receive operator wiring diagram and header reinforcement requirements; settled before frame fabrication.
25. Access control integrator (28 13 00): send electrified hardware schedule, wiring diagrams, device voltages/currents, and fail-safe/fail-secure designations; receive the integrator's door-by-door device list and a demarcation of who furnishes power supplies, REX, and door contacts; settled before low-voltage rough-in (prior to drywall).
26. Fire alarm (28 46 00): send list of doors with magnetic holders, electromagnetic locks, delayed egress, and electrified latch retraction requiring alarm release; receive relay/module locations and the sequence-of-operations matrix; settled before fire alarm shop drawings and before ceilings close.
27. Electrical (26 05 00): send 120 V circuit requirements and locations for every hardware power supply and auto operator, and junction box location/side at every electrified door; receive panel schedule circuit assignments and box locations on E-sheets; settled before electrical rough-in.
28. Framing/drywall (09 21 16): send locations needing in-wall blocking for wall stops and overhead stop reinforcement, and conduit stubs from frame to junction box; receive confirmation; settled before wall close-in.
29. Owner facilities/locksmith: hold the keying conference; receive keyway, master-key hierarchy, key quantities, core type, any existing key system to extend, and bitting records ownership; settled no later than 8–10 weeks before substantial completion so permanent cores arrive for turnover (earlier where phased occupancy is planned).
30. Flooring (09 60 00) and exterior paving: confirm finished floor elevations each side of thresholds and saddle widths; settled before thresholds are ordered and before slab pour at exterior doors.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

31. Hardware schedule index ↔ architect door schedule: door number, hand, swing, rating, size, door and frame material, hardware set number.
32. Hardware schedule ↔ hollow metal and wood door shop drawings: per door, hinge quantity/size/location, strike type and location, lock backset and prep (cylindrical vs. mortise), closer reinforcement, exit device type, EPT prep, raceway.
33. Electrified hardware wiring diagrams ↔ access control device schedule: door number, locking device type, fail-safe/fail-secure, operating voltage, current draw, REX present, door contact present, power supply ID.
34. Hardware schedule ↔ fire alarm sequence of operations: every magnetic holder, electromagnetic lock, delayed egress device, and electrified latch retraction appears in the FA matrix with release on alarm and on power loss.
35. Keying schedule ↔ hardware schedule: every cylinder-bearing item (locks, exit device trim, cylinder dogging, removable mullions, key switches, padlocks) has a keying line; cylinder type and keyway identical in both.
36. Hardware schedule ↔ code/life-safety plan: panic/fire exit hardware on every door shown as a required exit from an occupant load that triggers it, no special locking except where the code plan calls it out, stair re-entry doors unlocked or fail-safe.
37. Hardware schedule ↔ electrical drawings: count and location of 120 V circuits/junction boxes for power supplies and operators matches.
38. Product data ↔ hardware schedule: catalog numbers, finishes, functions, lever designs, and strike/option codes identical; rated-opening items ↔ listing cards show the same product and rating.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

39. Does every egress door unlatch with one motion, no key or special knowledge, from the egress side (IBC 1010.2 / NFPA 101 7.2.1.5)? Answer from memory; confirm adopted IBC edition and amendments with the building department (AHJ).
40. Which doors need panic or fire exit hardware by occupant load (IBC 1010.2.9: 50 or more in Group A/E, 100 or more in other groups; electrical rooms per NEC 110.26(C)(3))? Memory, checked against the code plan occupant loads.
41. Are classroom locks lockable from inside without a key, egressable in one motion, with the required visibility/indicator, and do they satisfy the state school-safety door-locking statute? Memory for IBC 1010.2.8; research the state statute and fire marshal position (barricade devices are usually prohibited).
42. Are electromagnetic locks with sensor release (IBC 1010.2.11) or door-hardware release (1010.2.12), and delayed egress (1010.2.13), permitted in this occupancy and provided with the required releases (loss of power, fire alarm, manual push button with 30 s release, signage)? Memory for the rules; research state amendments and the local fire marshal's acceptance testing practice.
43. Are operable parts 34"–48" AFF, operable without tight grasping/twisting, and with ≤ 5 lbf to operate (2010 ADA 404.2.7; ICC A117.1)? Memory.
44. Are opening forces ≤ 5 lbf on interior non-rated doors and closing sweep ≥ 5 s from 90° to 12° (ADA 404.2.8/404.2.9); do rated doors' latching forces exceed this such that the AHJ expects an auto operator? Memory for the rule; research the AHJ's practice on rated door force.
45. Are thresholds ≤ 1/2" with 1:2 bevel above 1/4" (ADA 404.2.5) and is the maneuvering clearance at the door consistent with the hardware side chosen? Memory.
46. Do fire door assemblies remain positive-latching, self-closing, with listed components and only permitted field preps (NFPA 80 §5.1.4, 3/4" max holes), and does the AHJ require an NFPA 80 annual inspection record at closeout? Memory for the rule; research the AHJ closeout requirement.
47. Are fire alarm interfaces to locking hardware done through listed control modules and is a release test required at final inspection (NFPA 72)? Memory; research the fire marshal's test form.
48. Is a power-operated door required at the accessible entrance by local code or owner standard (some jurisdictions and most school districts require ANSI/BHMA A156.19 low-energy operators at main entrances)? Research: local accessibility amendments and owner design standards.
49. Do stair doors in buildings over 4 stories need re-entry or fail-safe locking with FA release (IBC 1010.2.7)? Memory.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

50. Hardware schedule approved after frames are released; frames arrive with wrong strike prep or no EPT prep; field drilling/welding on rated frames voids the listing and the inspector tags the opening.
51. Handing reversed between the architect's schedule and the supplier's convention; closers, strikes, exit device trims, and handed levers arrive wrong; 6–10 week reorder.
52. No raceway or conduit from frame to junction box; cable surface-run or door left unlocked at turnover; conduit cut into finished walls.
53. Fail-safe/fail-secure swapped: stair doors trap occupants on power loss, or secured storage/IT rooms unlock on power loss.
54. Power supply located remotely with undersized conductors; voltage drop on 24 VDC runs causes strikes to chatter or latch retraction to fail intermittently.
55. Panic hardware with cylinder dogging or non-rated closer/holder installed on a rated door; fails fire inspection and must be replaced.
56. Keying conference held too late; building turned over on construction cores; owner rekeys at own cost and has a security gap during occupancy.
57. Mixed manufacturers in one opening without compatible templating (e.g., electrified lever from one maker with another maker's EPT); door never locks reliably and warranty is disputed.
58. Closer set to meet ADA opening force cannot overcome latch and gasketing on a rated or exterior door; door stands ajar, rating and security defeated.
59. Threshold set before floor finishes resolved; gap under door, trip lip over 1/2", failed accessibility inspection.
60. Overhead stop and closer both specified at the head without coordination; physical interference or stop limits closer arm travel.
61. Auto operator pushes before electric strike releases; door jams against strike, operator trips or burns out; sequencing relay was never specified.
62. Classroom locks that only lock from the corridor side by key; teachers cannot secure the room; district rejects at occupancy and all classroom locks are changed.
63. Hardware delivered loose rather than packaged by set and door number; items lost or installed on wrong doors; punch list becomes a re-order list.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

74. Office: tenant suite entry doors get electrified lever or strike with key override and REX, tenant-separation keying under a building master, stair re-entry per IBC 1010.2.7 on multi-story, and privacy-indicator locks on single-occupant toilets; verify base-building vs. tenant improvement hardware demarcation.
75. School (Group E): classroom security function with inside thumbturn and indicator on every classroom, exterior doors with exterior trim fixed except at the monitored main vestibule, intercom/remote release at the vestibule, lockdown mode integrated with access control, panic hardware on gym/cafeteria/assembly rooms, and no classroom barricade devices; confirm the district's keyway and lockdown standard at the keying conference.
76. Healthcare (Group I-2) changes: controlled egress locks (IBC 1010.2.14) permitted in patient care areas with FA/sprinkler release, cross-corridor smoke doors with magnetic holders released by FA, patient room doors with push/pull latches and positive latching per NFPA 101 Ch. 18/19, ICU breakaway doors with release hardware, heavy-weight hinges on lead-lined doors, antimicrobial or stainless finishes, and Joint Commission/CMS life-safety survey documentation; review keying for pharmacy/med rooms as a separate high-security system.
77. Behavioral health units: anti-ligature levers and pulls, continuous hinges with ligature-resistant covers, no closer arms or exposed hardware on the patient side, inward/outward double-swing patient room doors with staff override; every item must carry an anti-ligature test report, not just a catalog claim.
78. Phased or early-occupancy projects of any type: require permanent cores and keying for the occupied phase, temporary construction keying for the rest, and a written core-swap plan with dates tied to the certificate-of-occupancy schedule.
