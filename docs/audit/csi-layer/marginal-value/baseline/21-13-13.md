# 21 13 13 Wet-Pipe Sprinkler Systems — Submittal Review Checklist (contractor-designed)

Baseline project: K-12 school, Risk Category III, light hazard with ordinary-hazard pockets (kitchen, shops, stage, mechanical, storage). Healthcare and warehouse deltas are in Section 8.

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Working plans signed/stamped by the designer of record: NICET Level III/IV in fire sprinkler layout or a licensed PE as the state and the spec require; state fire-sprinkler contractor license number on every sheet.
2. Working plans carry every NFPA 13 Chapter 28 (2019/2022 ed.) item: scale, north arrow, occupancy and hazard class per area, ceiling heights and construction, full-height sections, pipe sizes/lengths/elevations, hanger and brace locations, sprinkler model/K/temp/orientation per area, design-area outlines, water-supply data with test date, location and elevation.
3. Hydraulic calculation package: summary sheet per remote area (hazard, density, area, hose allowance inside/outside, demand at base of riser and at point of supply), supply-vs-demand graph with the spec safety margin shown numerically, and node-by-node printout (pipe ID, C-factor, equivalent lengths, friction loss, elevation, velocity) keyed to a node diagram.
4. Water flow test report: date (typically within 12 months), hydrants used, static/residual/flow, gauge elevation relative to riser, witness, time of day; or pump curve / tank data when applicable.
5. Riser diagram and riser detail: supervised control valve, check/alarm valve, main drain, gauges, inspector's test connection location, FDC with check valve and ball drip, flow switch, pressure switch, backflow preventer position, drain discharge point.
6. Product data with the exact model marked for: pipe (standard, schedule, finish), fittings and grooved couplings, hangers/rods/inserts/anchors, sway braces, sprinklers (each SIN), control valves, check/alarm valves, FDC, backflow preventer, flow and tamper switches, gauges, escutcheons/cover plates, head guards, spare-head cabinet.
7. UL listing or FM approval shown for each product in the configuration used (e.g., concealed head listed with that specific cover plate; coupling listed for the pipe and service).
8. Sprinkler legend/schedule: manufacturer, SIN, K-factor, orifice, temperature rating, response type, finish, deflector type, with a count per area tied to the spare-head quantities (6/12/24 per NFPA 13).
9. Seismic bracing package when SDC C or higher (Ip = 1.5 for a school): brace locations on plan, load calcs per NFPA 13 Ch. 18, listed brace components, anchor type and embedment, flexible couplings at riser, building seismic joints and where pipe crosses between structures.
10. Pipe/material schedule stating what is used where (e.g., Sch 40 threaded ≤2", Sch 10 grooved ≥2-1/2", CPVC only in listed locations) and joining method per size.
11. AHJ submittal evidence: transmittal to the fire marshal (state fire marshal for many public schools) with permit/plan-review number; commitment to issue the AHJ-stamped set to the GC when returned.
12. Welding credentials if shop-welded outlets are used: WPS/PQR and welder qualifications to ASME IX / AWS D10.12; fabricator name.
13. Reference to closeout forms: NFPA 13 Contractor's Material and Test Certificate (aboveground) and NFPA 24 underground certificate, and the hydrostatic test plan (200 psi / 2 hr, or 50 psi over max static when >150 psi).

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

14. Hazard classification per room on the working plans vs. the spec basis-of-design table and the architect's room schedule/code plan: classrooms, corridors, offices = LH; kitchen, mechanical, shops, stage = OH1/OH2; storage over 12 ft piled = check whether it is storage occupancy.
15. Density/area per remote area vs. NFPA 13 curves and any spec override; the quick-response area reduction (down to ~1,050 sf at ≤10 ft ceilings) is only valid for wet systems, QR heads throughout the compartment, ceilings ≤20 ft and no unsprinklered combustible concealed spaces.
16. Remote-area selection: confirm the calculated area is actually the governing one (highest elevation, farthest from riser, the OH pocket, the gridded area with peaking check); require a calc for each hazard class and for any area fed through small pipe.
17. Water supply in the calc equals the flow test (static, residual, flow, elevation correction); hose allowance 100 gpm LH / 250 gpm OH; safety margin meets spec (commonly 10% or 5 psi, whichever the spec says); 30-minute LH / 60–90-minute OH duration if a tank is involved.
18. C-factors and internal diameters match the pipe schedule: 120 black steel, 150 CPVC/copper, 140 DI underground cement-lined, 150 PVC underground; Sch 10 vs Sch 40 ID actually entered.
19. Velocity cap per spec (often 20 fps aboveground, 10 fps underground) checked in the printout; flag runs over the cap.
20. Backflow preventer headloss at design flow taken from the manufacturer's curve and entered in the calc, plus meter loss if the fire service is metered.
21. Pipe standards vs. spec: ASTM A53 Type E/S or A795 (not A53 Type F), A135; threaded only on Sch 40 (Sch 10 may not be threaded); galvanized where spec requires for dry/exposed areas; CPVC only where spec, listing (LH, concealed or protected) and compatibility allow.
22. Fittings and joints: ASME B16.4/B16.3 threaded, listed grooved couplings/fittings, listed mechanical tees; rigid vs flexible couplings consistent with the bracing design; no plain-end or unlisted outlet fittings.
23. Hangers: listed per NFPA 13 Ch. 17; spacing (12 ft for 1" steel, 15 ft for 1-1/4" and larger steel; CPVC per listing); rod size per pipe size; concrete anchors qualified for cracked concrete; powder-actuated fasteners only where spec and listing permit and never for seismic loads; no attachment to metal deck alone.
24. Sprinkler coverage and obstructions on the plan: spacing within listing (225 sf LH / 130 sf OH standard spray; EC per listing), 3-times rule and beam rule, sprinklers below ducts and soffits wider than 48", under stage, under open mezzanine/catwalks, in combustible concealed spaces unless filled or sealed, under exterior canopies over 4 ft of combustible construction.
25. Sprinkler types and finish vs. spec and finish schedule: concealed in finished ceilings, QR in all LH, intermediate-temp at kitchen hoods, unit heaters, skylights and attics; corrosion-resistant in pool/locker rooms; guards in gyms, locker rooms, mechanical rooms; dry sidewalls or dry pendents for coolers, canopies and loading docks.
26. Riser hardware vs. spec: OS&Y or indicating butterfly with tamper switch, main drain sized per NFPA 13 (2" for risers over 4"), inspector's test at the hydraulically remote point with sight glass or discharge outside, pressure gauges upstream/downstream of check valve, FDC sized and threaded per the fire department.
27. Scope completeness vs. the code plan: no omitted areas (elevator machine room/hoistway/pit per ASME A17.1 and IBC 3005, electrical rooms unless the spec documents the 2-hr/dry-equipment exemption, telecom rooms, exterior canopies, overhangs, crawl spaces, attics).
28. Pipe routing vs. structural drawings: no beam penetrations without the EOR's approval, mains below joist bottom chord or between joists per hanger detail, no hangers from bridging or deck.
29. FDC, PIV, riser room and backflow locations on the working plans vs. civil site plan, architectural plans and fire-department access requirements (FDC within the department's hose distance of a hydrant, visible, not behind landscaping).
30. Freeze exposure: every pipe segment in an unheated space (attic, canopy, vestibule, dock) is dry sidewall, dry system (other section), or listed heat tracing with insulation; antifreeze only as a listed premixed solution, which on a school I would reject.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

31. Structural (03 30 00 / 05 12 00 / 05 21 00): send hanger and brace attachment details with loads and anchor types; need back EOR acceptance of attachment points and any beam/joist penetrations; settle before steel fabrication release and before the first deck pour.
32. HVAC (23 31 00 duct): exchange BIM models; identify every duct over 48" wide and soffit requiring sprinklers beneath, confirm elevations in corridors; settle at coordination sign-off, before overhead rough-in in that area.
33. Fire alarm (28 31 00) and electrical (26 05 00): send device list (flow switch with retard, tamper switches on every control valve including backflow, pressure switch at FDC/riser, bell location); need back FA shop drawings showing the monitoring points and power; settle before FA submittal approval and before wall rough-in in the riser room.
34. Civil / site utilities (33 11 00 / 21 11 00): point of connection, service size, meter/backflow location (vault vs. inside), thrust restraint, flushing and 200 psi underground test witnessed before the riser is connected; settle before underground installation.
35. Plumbing (22 11 00): main drain and inspector's test discharge, RPZ relief discharge drain capacity, riser-room floor drain; settle before slab-on-grade underground plumbing.
36. Architectural / ceilings (09 51 00, 09 29 00): heads centered in tile, cover plate colors, soffit coverage, head placement in exposed-structure spaces; need back updated RCPs; settle before ceiling grid starts.
37. Kitchen equipment (11 40 00) and hood suppression (21 20 00 / UL 300): intermediate-temp heads around hoods, no sprinklers inside hoods, interface to shunt gas and alarm; settle before hood submittal approval.
38. Elevator (14 21 00): confirm sprinklers in machine room/pit if provided, heat detectors for shunt trip within 2 ft of each head, 2-hr fire-rated feeder if sprinklered; settle before elevator submittal approval.
39. Water purveyor and owner's insurer (FM Global, if applicable): backflow assembly type and USC approval, cross-connection inspection, FM data sheet requirements that override NFPA 13 (e.g., FM-approved pipe and heads); settle before the sprinkler submittal is finalized.
40. Firestopping (07 84 00) and roofing: listed through-penetration systems for steel and CPVC pipe at rated walls and floors (CPVC-compatible sealants only), FDC and signage on the facade; settle before rough-in through rated walls.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

41. Sprinkler legend on working plans ↔ sprinkler product data: SIN, K-factor, orifice, temperature rating, response type, finish, deflector/orientation.
42. Hydraulic calc node diagram ↔ working plan: node numbers, pipe sizes, pipe lengths, fitting counts and types, elevations, head count in the design area.
43. Calc summary sheet ↔ design-area outline on plans: location, number of heads flowing, area in sf, density, hazard class.
44. Calc water supply inputs ↔ flow test report: static, residual, flow, hydrant location, test elevation, date.
45. Calc pipe table (ID, C-factor) ↔ pipe/material schedule and pipe product data (schedule and standard).
46. Backflow preventer product data headloss curve ↔ loss entered in the calc; valve size ↔ underground service size on civil.
47. Hanger schedule ↔ hanger/anchor product data listings (rod size, pipe size range, anchor type) ↔ structural attachment detail approved by EOR.
48. Seismic brace calc loads and spacing ↔ plan brace locations ↔ brace product listed capacity ↔ project SDC and Ip on structural general notes.
49. Hazard classification per area on working plans ↔ code analysis / life-safety plan occupancy ↔ spec basis-of-design table.
50. Riser detail device count ↔ fire alarm device list and FA shop drawings.
51. FDC, PIV and riser location on working plans ↔ civil utility plan ↔ architectural floor and elevation drawings.
52. AHJ-stamped set ↔ GC-reviewed set: any AHJ comments incorporated and a reconciled issue distributed to all trades.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

53. Which NFPA 13 edition is enforced through the adopted IBC/IFC for this jurisdiction, and does the spec cite a different edition? Research (state fire marshal / local amendments); never assume from memory.
54. Does the state require sprinkler working plans to be sealed by a PE, or is NICET III/IV acceptable, and must the contractor hold a state fire-sprinkler license? Research the state licensing board.
55. Who is the plan-review AHJ for a public school in this state (state fire marshal, local fire marshal, state school-facilities agency) and is approval required before installation? Research.
56. What backflow assembly does the water purveyor require on a fire service (DCVA vs. RPZ vs. detector assembly), must it be on the USC FCCCHR approved list, and where may it be installed? Research with the purveyor.
57. Does the AHJ accept a flow test older than 6 or 12 months, and must the test be witnessed by the fire department or purveyor? Research locally.
58. Is the project in SDC C or higher such that NFPA 13 bracing applies (Ip = 1.5, Risk Category III for a school over 250 occupants)? Answer from memory using ASCE 7 Ch. 13, confirmed against the structural general notes.
59. Does IBC 903.2.3 trigger NFPA 13 for this E occupancy (over 12,000 sf fire area, below exit discharge, or above the level of exit discharge), and are any of the 903.3.1.1.1 exemptions (electrical rooms, non-combustible concealed spaces) being claimed? Memory, then check with the AHJ on the exemption.
60. Does the local fire department require a specific FDC connection (2-1/2" NST siamese vs. 4"/5" Storz), location, signage and Knox caps? Research the department's standard.
61. Does the hydraulic result indicate a fire pump is needed once safety margin and hose demand are applied, and if so is the project scope (21 30 00) and electrical service ready for it? Memory for the method; the answer comes from the calc.
62. Is the elevator machine room/hoistway required to be sprinklered under the adopted ASME A17.1 and IBC 3005, and does the AHJ accept omitting them with a 2-hr enclosure? Memory for the rule; confirm with the elevator inspector.
63. Does the state cross-connection program require a certified installer/tester for the backflow assembly and a test report at acceptance? Research.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

64. Remote area chosen for convenience rather than hydraulic demand; AHJ or peer reviewer rejects the calc after pipe is fabricated, leading to upsized mains or a fire pump nobody budgeted.
65. Flow test taken at the wrong hydrant, at night, or at a lower elevation than the riser without correction; paper margin disappears at acceptance test, system fails demand.
66. Ducts over 48" wide and soffits installed before sprinkler coordination; sprinklers below them added as a change order with no ceiling access left.
67. Hangers attached to metal deck, bridging or joist web members not approved by the EOR; structural rejects at inspection and hangers are reinstalled over finished ceilings.
68. 155°F quick-response heads installed near unit heaters, kitchen hoods or skylights; nuisance activations and water damage in the first summer.
69. Concealed cover plates painted by the finish painter, or cover plates from another manufacturer; fails final inspection, every plate replaced.
70. CPVC contacted by incompatible firestop, cutting oil, flexible-duct sealant or antimicrobial-coated steel pipe; environmental stress cracking leaks months after occupancy.
71. Backflow preventer sized by pipe size rather than headloss; loss at demand is 10–15 psi more than assumed and margin is gone.
72. Flow switch retard not set and FA not coordinated; false alarms on pressure surges, fire department stops responding to real alarms.
73. Underground not flushed before connecting the riser; gravel and debris plug the inspector's test and the smaller heads, found at the main-drain test.
74. Pipe left full after hydro test in an attic or canopy before heat is on; freeze break and water damage to finished classrooms.
75. Combustible concealed spaces (wood-framed soffits, sloped ceilings with wood joists) missed on the plans; AHJ final inspection fails, opening finished drywall.
76. Flexible couplings omitted at the riser and building seismic joints, or braces missing in the last 10% of the installation; seismic inspection fails on a school with Ip 1.5.
77. Elevator shunt-trip heat detectors not installed within 2 ft of the heads in the machine room; elevator state inspection fails and delays the certificate of occupancy.
78. Contractor submits to the AHJ before GC/engineer review; AHJ approves a set with wrong hazards or finishes, and the AHJ resubmittal costs weeks.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

94. K-12 school (baseline): Risk Category III drives Ip = 1.5 bracing; head guards in gyms, locker rooms, shops and anywhere students can reach; institutional (tamper-resistant) concealed heads in corridors; corrosion-resistant heads in pools and showers; stage and shop areas are OH2; check the state school-facilities agency as a second AHJ.
95. Healthcare (hospital, I-2): NFPA 101 and CMS/Joint Commission apply — QR heads required throughout patient sleeping areas; zone the system by smoke compartment with a supervised control valve and flow switch per zone so a shutdown never takes out more than one compartment; isolation valves must support the owner's ILSM/impairment program; MRI suites need non-ferrous heads and piping; no heads in sterile processing without the architect's finish review; Risk Category IV, Ip = 1.5, and often an emergency-powered fire pump.
96. Healthcare also changes the pipe: owners often require galvanized or internally coated pipe and a corrosion monitoring/nitrogen program in long-life wings; verify the spec's corrosion package and reject plain black Sch 10 if the spec calls for it.
97. Warehouse (S-1/S-2 storage): the review starts with commodity classification, storage method (palletized, rack, solid pile), storage height and ceiling height from the owner — if the owner has not stated these in writing, stop the review; the design is either CMDA density/area for storage, CMSA, or ESFR (K-14 to K-25.2 at 50–75 psi with roof slope ≤ 2/12, no heat/smoke vents unless listed), and hose demand rises to 500 gpm for 2 hours.
98. Warehouse also changes the hardware: a fire pump is nearly certain, in-rack sprinklers and listed solid-shelf limits must be coordinated with the racking vendor (whose shop drawings become a sprinkler-coordination document), draft curtains and obstructions below ESFR heads (joists, lights, conveyors) are checked against the ESFR obstruction rules, and insurer (FM) data sheets usually govern over NFPA 13.
