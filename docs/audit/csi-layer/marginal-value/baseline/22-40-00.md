# 22 40 00 Plumbing Fixtures — Submittal Review Checklist (K-12 baseline, healthcare deltas noted)

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Transmittal identifies section 22 40 00, submittal number/revision, and whether the package is complete or a partial (fixtures, carriers, drains, and trim are often split across three packages; log which tags are still open).
2. Fixture cross-reference matrix: every schedule tag (WC-1, L-1, U-1, SK-1, EWC-1, FD-1, MS-1, etc.) mapped to manufacturer, model, color/finish, and every component that makes the tag whole: fixture, faucet or flush valve, carrier, seat, supplies/stops, P-trap, drain/strainer, insulation kit, mixing valve.
3. Cut sheet for each component with the exact ordered model number, suffixes and options circled, inapplicable options struck; a cut sheet with nothing marked is incomplete.
4. Rough-in drawing per fixture and per carrier: supply/waste centerlines and heights, rim height, anchor-bolt pattern, allowable finished-wall thickness range, stud/chase depth required.
5. Flush valves: gpf, manual vs. sensor, power (battery, hardwired transformer, hydro-generator), handle side, vandal-resistant stop cap.
6. Faucets: gpm and aerator vs. laminar outlet, actuation (sensor, metering, manual), supply hose/connection size, power, integral or separate thermostatic mixing device.
7. Carriers: horizontal/vertical, single/back-to-back, foot-supported vs. wall-anchored, load rating, waste outlet size and offset, fit to the project wall depth.
8. Floor drains/floor sinks: body material, outlet size and connection type, top size/shape/finish, membrane clamp, trap primer tapping, load class, sediment bucket where specified.
9. Thermostatic mixing valves: ASSE 1070 at lavs/sinks (point-of-use) and ASSE 1017 at master, with set-point range and flow curve matching the fixture gpm.
10. ADA undersink insulation kit (ASME A112.18.9) listed for every accessible lav and sink.
11. Electric water coolers/bottle fillers: bi-level ADA configuration, filter and lead-reduction listing (NSF 42/53), electrical load, drain requirement for the filler.
12. Lead-content and health-effects listings (NSF/ANSI/CAN 372 and 61) for every potable-contact fitting and fixture.
13. Water-efficiency listings (WaterSense) where the spec or jurisdiction requires them.
14. Seats: open-front, check-hinge or heavy-duty, color match.
15. Supplies and stops: quarter-turn, loose-key vs. handle, finish; braided flexible connectors listed to ASME A112.18.6.
16. Warranty terms, called out separately for electronics (sensor modules, solenoids, batteries typically carry a shorter warranty than the china).
17. Finish/color sample if anything other than the manufacturer's standard white.
18. Any substitution flagged on the transmittal with the Division 01 substitution request form, including a side-by-side rough-in comparison to basis-of-design.

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

19. Compare each tag against both the plumbing fixture schedule (P-sheets) and the 22 40 00 fixture article; where the two disagree, resolve via Division 01 precedence and get the engineer's written answer before approving.
20. Count tags on the plumbing floor plans and compare to the matrix quantity to catch unscheduled fixtures (classroom sinks, kiln-room sinks, concession hand sinks are the usual misses).
21. Check rim heights and centerlines against architectural interior elevations and the accessibility mounting-height sheet; for K-12 confirm which rooms use children's heights by grade.
22. Accessible WC: centerline 16–18 in. (12–18 in. for children's), flush valve handle on the open/wide side, seat height 17–19 in. adult (11–17 in. children's).
23. Accessible lav/sink: knee clearance 27 in. high at 8 in. deep, 29 in. under apron, 34 in. max rim; faucet operable with one hand, under 5 lbf, no tight grasping; metering faucets hold open 10 s minimum.
24. Verify flow/flush rates against spec and the governing water-efficiency table (typical 1.28 gpf WC, 0.125 or 0.5 gpf urinal, 0.5 gpm public lav, 2.2 gpm sinks, 1.8 gpm showers).
25. Compare carrier allowable wall thickness and stud depth against architectural wall-type schedule and chase-wall framing details; back-to-back carriers in 6 in. metal stud chases are the common failure.
26. Compare carrier anchor footprint against structural slab details (slab-on-grade thickness, post-tension tendon zones, depressed slabs).
27. Compare floor drain top type and finish to the room finish schedule (tile insert/square top in tile, nickel-bronze in finished areas, cast iron in mechanical rooms) and slab slope details.
28. Verify sensor faucet/flush valve power type against electrical plans (120 V box locations and transformer count); if the drawings show nothing, battery is the de facto design and the owner must accept that.
29. Confirm supply connection sizes against the domestic water plan/riser (1 in. for flush-valve WC, 3/4 in. for flush-valve urinal, 1/2 in. lavs) and that water hammer arrestors in 22 11 19 are sized for the flush-valve groups.
30. Verify fixture materials for the room type: vitreous china in restrooms, stainless in kitchens and art rooms, epoxy resin or acid-resistant in science rooms.
31. Science room sinks and faucets: confirm who furnishes (12 35 53 casework vs. plumber), vacuum-breaker faucets, and acid-waste piping tie-in in 22 66 00.
32. Kitchen fixtures: compare against the food service equipment schedule (11 40 00/FS sheets) for hand sinks, 3-compartment sink, pre-rinse, and indirect waste to floor sinks.
33. Service/mop sink faucet with integral vacuum breaker, hose thread, pail hook, and wall brace; mop sink with stainless rim guard where specified.
34. Trap primer locations against every floor drain; confirm primer type (ASSE 1018 pressure-drop or ASSE 1044 electronic) matches what the engineer drew.
35. Showers (locker rooms): ASSE 1016 pressure-balance/thermostatic valves, vandal-resistant heads, and that flow matches the water heater sizing basis.
36. For any substitution, overlay the substitute's rough-in on the basis-of-design rough-in and confirm supply/waste positions are within tolerance of the already-roughed piping.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

37. Cold-formed framing (05 40 00/09 22 16) and wood blocking (06 10 00): send carrier anchor patterns, wall-hung lav/sink blocking locations, and chase depths; need back marked-up framing shop drawings; settle before chase-wall framing starts.
38. Concrete (03 30 00): send floor drain/floor sink locations with outlet elevations, carrier foot anchor points, and slab depressions/sleeves; need back pour-sequence drawings with sleeves located; settle before slab-on-grade and each elevated pour.
39. Electrical (26 05 33/26 27 26): send list of sensor fixtures, water coolers, electronic trap primers with voltage, transformer counts and box locations; need back circuit assignments and box locations on their rough-in; settle before wall close-in.
40. Tile/flooring (09 30 00/09 65 00): send drain top types and heights and the finish floor thickness assumed; need back finish thickness per room and slope-to-drain confirmation; settle before slab finishing/drain setting.
41. Toilet accessories (10 28 00): send fixture centerlines and flush handle sides; need back grab bar and dispenser layouts that clear the valve and carrier; settle before blocking.
42. Toilet partitions (10 21 13): send WC centerlines and urinal spacing; need back partition shop drawings with pilaster locations clearing carriers and urinal screens; settle before partition shop approval.
43. Food service (11 40 00): send plumbing fixture schedule tags in the kitchen; need back a furnished-by/installed-by matrix and final connection sizes; settle before underground plumbing.
44. Lab casework (12 35 53): send science sink/faucet tags; need back casework shop drawings showing sink cutouts and furnished-by; settle before casework shop approval.
45. Domestic water piping and heaters (22 11 00/22 33 00): send mixing valve set points and fixture gpm totals; need back confirmation of master mixing valve sizing and recirculation routing; settle before water heater submittal approval.
46. HVAC (23 00 00): send floor drain locations in mechanical rooms; need back condensate and relief discharge points landing on drains with air gaps; settle before overhead rough-in.
47. Owner facilities staff: send manufacturer list for district standardization (parts stocking, flush valve rebuild kits, sensor modules); need back acceptance or a direction to match district standard; settle before approval, because it drives a substitution either way.
48. Architect/accessibility reviewer: send mounting-height sheet with grade-level assignments per restroom; need back signed confirmation; settle before framing.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

49. Fixture schedule (drawings) vs. 22 40 00 spec fixture article: tag, manufacturer, model, gpf/gpm, finish.
50. Fixture schedule vs. submittal matrix: every tag present, same model numbers, same component list.
51. Submittal matrix vs. each cut sheet: model number including suffixes and options.
52. Rough-in drawing vs. architectural mounting-height sheet/interior elevations: rim height, centerline from side wall, grab bar relationship.
53. Rough-in drawing vs. plumbing plan and riser: supply/waste sizes and positions, trap arm length.
54. Carrier cut sheet vs. wall-type schedule and framing shop drawings: wall thickness range, stud depth, anchor pattern.
55. Floor drain cut sheet vs. finish schedule and slab details: top size/finish, membrane clamp, outlet size and elevation.
56. Sensor fixture cut sheets vs. electrical plans: voltage, transformer quantity, box locations.
57. Food service equipment schedule vs. plumbing fixture schedule: who furnishes each sink and faucet, connection sizes.
58. Toilet partition shop drawings vs. fixture rough-in: WC centerlines, urinal spacing, pilaster locations.
59. Mixing valve cut sheet vs. water heater schedule/riser: supply temperature, set point, flow range.
60. Submittal matrix quantity vs. the plumber's purchase order (internal check before release, especially for color and handle-side variants).

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

61. Which plumbing code and edition has the AHJ adopted (IPC vs. UPC, with state amendments)? Research the state/local adoption; it changes fixture counts, trap primer and mixing valve rules.
62. Do the fixture counts meet the occupant-load minimums for Group E occupancy in the adopted code table? Engineer's responsibility, but spot-check from memory and flag if a restroom was deleted during VE.
63. Which accessibility standard governs: 2010 ADA Standards, ICC A117.1 (which edition), or a state code (CBC 11B, Texas TAS)? Research the adopted version; the children's-height exception ranges I answer from memory and confirm with the architect.
64. Does the state require lead testing, filtration, or certified filtered bottle fillers at drinking outlets in schools? Research; several states now mandate it and it changes the water cooler spec.
65. Does the state mandate flow/flush limits stricter than federal (e.g., California, Colorado, Washington, Georgia)? Research the state plumbing/efficiency statute.
66. Are all potable-contact fittings lead-free to the federal 0.25 percent weighted limit (SDWA 1417, NSF 372)? Answer from memory: yes, mandatory, and verify the listing on each cut sheet.
67. Is a point-of-use ASSE 1070 device required at every public lav and hand sink? Answer from memory: yes under IPC 416.5/UPC 407.3; confirm the engineer provided it rather than relying on the master valve.
68. Are trap seal protection devices required at each floor drain? Answer from memory: yes under IPC 1002.4/UPC 1007; verify the method shown.
69. Are hose-threaded outlets (mop sink, hose bibbs) protected by ASSE 1011 or 1052 backflow devices? Answer from memory: yes; verify the faucet model has it integral.
70. Does the health department's retail food code (state version of FDA Food Code) require hand sink spacing, hands-free operation, or specific splash guards in the kitchen? Research the local health authority's plan review requirements.
71. Does the state school facility authority (e.g., California CDE/DSA, Texas TEA) impose fixture type or vandal-resistance rules beyond the model code? Research.
72. If LEED or CALGreen water-use reduction is targeted, does the submitted fixture mix hit the calculated baseline reduction? Research the project's registered credit calc and rerun with submitted values.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

73. Carriers approved before chase framing was coordinated; back-to-back carrier won't fit a 6 in. chase, walls get furred, corridor/room dimensions shrink, and door frames and partitions have to be re-ordered.
74. Carrier feet set to a guessed finish floor elevation; tile thickness changes and every WC rim ends up 1/2 to 1 in. off, failing the accessibility inspection and forcing carrier re-sets in finished walls.
75. Floor drains set before finish floor elevation was fixed; drains sit high and the floor puddles, or sit low and the tile is feathered around them.
76. Sensor fixtures approved as hardwired with no electrical boxes on the electrical set; field reverts to batteries, and the district inherits a battery-replacement program they never agreed to.
77. Substituted fixture with different rough-in dimensions approved on a cut sheet alone; at trim-out the supply stubs land behind the apron or the waste misses the carrier outlet, and holes get cored in finished tile.
78. Flush valve handles on the wall side of accessible stalls; inspector fails the restroom and every valve is swapped.
79. Adult mounting heights used throughout a K-5 building, or children's heights applied in staff restrooms; both get cited.
80. Kitchen sinks assumed furnished by food service and also assumed by plumber; nobody orders them, or the faucet connections don't match the sink deck.
81. ASSE 1070 valves omitted because the master mixing valve was "good enough"; scald risk and failed final inspection.
82. Trap primers omitted or cut in VE; dry floor drains and sewer gas complaints in the first month of occupancy.
83. Undersink insulation kits left off the approved submittal; cited at the accessibility walkthrough, long lead for color-matched kits.
84. Fixtures delivered in bone/biscuit instead of white because the suffix was wrong on the cut sheet; 8–12 week replacement.
85. Sensor modules and specialty flush valves on 16+ week lead; approval dragged and trim-out slides past the move-in date.
86. Water cooler recess and electrical not coordinated; cooler projects more than 4 in. into the corridor and becomes a protruding-object violation.
87. Bottle filler drain not piped; condensate and spill water onto the floor.
88. Standard-duty trim in a middle school; handles snapped, aerators stolen, and seats cracked within a semester because vandal-resistant options were not enforced at review.
89. Wall-hung lavs installed on blocking that was never put in; sinks sag, trap joints weep.
90. Urinal spacing set from the fixture schedule without the urinal screens; screens can't be installed without covering the flush valve stop.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

115. K-12 by grade band: pre-K/K classroom toilet rooms with 10–12 in. seat heights and 24 in. lav rims; elementary at children's accessibility ranges; middle/high at adult; staff and nurse rooms always adult. Get the grade map before checking a single height.
116. K-12 vandal resistance: tamper-proof stop caps and screws, concealed or vandal-resistant flush valve covers, metered or sensor faucets, vandal-resistant aerators and shower heads, solid-plastic or stainless seats, wall-hung china on heavy carriers.
117. K-12 drinking water: filtered bottle fillers with filter-change indicators, lead testing/flushing program compatibility, and bi-level units at each required location.
118. K-12 specialty rooms: science (acid-resistant sinks, vacuum-breaker gooseneck faucets, eyewash), art (plaster/solids interceptor sinks), kiln and shop rooms (hand sinks), kitchen (NSF-listed stainless, 3-compartment with grease interceptor tie-in), concessions, athletics (group showers, team-room sinks).
119. K-12 district standards usually override basis-of-design for flush valves and faucets so maintenance stocks one rebuild kit; treat a non-standard brand as a substitution even if it meets spec.
120. Healthcare (FGI Guidelines) hand-washing: hands-free (sensor or wrist/elbow blade, 4 in. min.) faucets, deep bowls, no overflow, offset drains so the stream does not hit the drain, laminar flow instead of aerators; recheck every lav in the package against this.
121. Healthcare scald and Legionella: ASSE 1070 at every hand-washing station, ASSE 1017 master valves, recirculation to within a few feet of each fixture; submittal must show set points consistent with the water management program (ASHRAE 188).
122. Healthcare fixture types not seen in schools: clinical/flushing-rim sinks, bedpan washers, scrub sinks with knee or foot controls, patient-room WC with 500 lb carrier (bariatric 1,000 lb and floor-mounted), roll-in showers with hand-held heads and thermostatic valves, dialysis box connections.
123. Healthcare drains: floor drains prohibited or restricted in ORs and many procedure rooms, flushing-rim drains where used, no floor drains in protective-environment rooms; trap primer type and location get infection-control review.
124. Behavioral health: ligature-resistant faucets, flush valves and WCs (no exposed trap, sloped tops), stainless or solid-surface fixtures, tamper-resistant supplies; every patient-accessible fixture is re-reviewed against the facility's ligature-risk assessment.
125. Healthcare surfaces: non-porous, integral-bowl solid surface or stainless at hand-washing stations; vitreous china with exposed trim is acceptable only in public/staff restrooms.
