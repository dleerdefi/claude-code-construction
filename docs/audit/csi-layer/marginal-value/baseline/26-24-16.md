# 26 24 16 Panelboards — Submittal Review Checklist (K-12 baseline; healthcare notes in Section 8)

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Manufacturer and product line for each panel class (lighting/appliance branch panel vs. power distribution panel, e.g. Square D NQ/NF vs. I-Line, Eaton PRL1a/2a vs. PRL3E/4, Siemens P1/P2 vs. P3/P4), with cut sheets marked to show the options furnished and unused options struck.
2. Panel-by-panel data sheet: designation exactly as on the drawings, voltage/phase/wire, main bus ampacity, main device (MLO or MCB with frame and trip), number of spaces, mounting (surface/flush), enclosure NEMA type, feed entry (top/bottom), and fully-rated vs. series-rated SCCR.
3. Branch breaker schedule per panel: circuit number, poles, trip, frame, breaker type (thermal-magnetic, electronic trip, GFCI 5 mA, GFEP 30 mA, AFCI, HID- or SWD-rated, HACR, shunt-trip, 100%-rated, lock-on/lock-off), and the load description copied from the drawing schedule.
4. Dimensional drawings per panel: box H x W x D, trim/door type, gutter widths, knockout-free zones, weight, and whether 60/84-circuit or column-width panels ship as one can or as sections.
5. Bus data: copper or aluminum, tin or silver plating, bus bracing in kA, neutral bus rating (100% or 200%), isolated-ground bar where scheduled, ground bar lug count and wire range.
6. Short-circuit data: UL 67 panel SCCR with the actual main and branch devices installed; if series-rated, the manufacturer's UL-recognized series combination table with the upstream device identified by catalog number.
7. Breaker interrupting ratings per frame at the panel's system voltage (kAIC at 208Y/120 and at 480Y/277 are different numbers for the same frame; the table must show the one that applies).
8. Main, neutral, and ground lug data: wire range (min-max AWG/kcmil), conductors per lug (double lugs for parallel feeders), AL/CU rating, and feed-through or sub-feed lug kits where the one-line shows a panel feeding another panel.
9. Nameplate and directory schedule: engraved nameplate text (designation, voltage, "fed from"), typed directory format, and the arc-flash label if the spec puts labels on the contractor instead of the study engineer.
10. Accessories called out by spec or drawings: integral or sidecar SPD (with its own UL 1449 data and disconnect), handle padlock attachments for fire alarm/elevator circuits, door-in-door trim, door locks keyed alike with the switchboard, hinged trim, sub-feed breakers, contactors, factory branch-circuit metering with protocol and CT list.
11. Listing statements: UL 67 panel listing, UL 489 breaker listing, enclosure UL 50/50E type, and seismic certification (ICC-ES AC156 / OSHPD OSP where required) when the project SDS and Ip trigger it.
12. Lead time per panel group and the subcontractor's own review stamp on the transmittal (an unstamped pass-through from the sub gets returned without review).

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

13. Walk the one-line diagram (E-6xx) and the drawing panel schedules (E-6xx/E-7xx) and tick off every panel designation; any panel on the drawings missing from the package, or any extra panel in the package, becomes a numbered comment.
14. Compare each panel's voltage/phase/wire against the one-line and the transformer or switchboard section feeding it.
15. Compare main bus amps and main breaker trip against the feeder tag on the one-line and the conductor size in the feeder schedule; main device must not exceed the feeder ampacity except as 240.4(B) next-size-up allows.
16. Compare every branch breaker trip and pole count against the drawing panel schedule; count spares and spaces against the spec's spare-capacity clause (K-12 specs typically require 20-25% spares plus spaces).
17. Compare SCCR/AIC per panel against the available fault current from the 26 05 73 short-circuit study or, if the study is not issued, against the AIC tags on the one-line; if the one-line says "fully rated" and the package shows series ratings, reject.
18. Check bus material against the spec paragraph (many district standards require copper bus and copper ground bar); confirm "copper" appears in the panel data line, not just as an option on the cut sheet.
19. Check 200% neutral where the drawing schedule or notes call it out (computer labs, LED lighting panels, kitchen panels).
20. Check enclosure type against location on the plans: NEMA 1 interior, NEMA 3R rooftop/site lighting/portable-classroom feeds, NEMA 12 in shop/kitchen where specified; check surface vs. flush against the plan symbol and the wall type at that location.
21. Check can dimensions against the actual wall: a flush 20"-wide can does not fit a 16" o.c. stud bay without a header detail, a 5-3/4" deep can does not fit a 3-5/8" stud wall, and recessed cans in rated walls need the architect's box-out detail; check working clearance (110.26) and door swing in the electrical room layout using the real can width.
22. Check breaker types against drawing schedule notes and 210.8(B): GFCI for kitchen, exterior, rooftop, and within 6 ft of sinks (science labs, art rooms, staff lounges); GFEP for heat trace; HACR for mechanical units; SWD/HID for lighting circuits; shunt-trip where kitchen hood suppression or elevator pit sprinklers require it.
23. Check lug wire ranges against the feeder schedule conductor sizes and quantities (a 400 A panel fed by parallel 500 kcmil needs two-conductor lugs; a 225 A panel fed by 4/0 CU is fine, fed by 350 kcmil AL needs the larger lug kit).
24. Check the manufacturer against the spec's acceptable manufacturer list and the Division 26 single-source clause (switchboard, panelboards, and breakers from one manufacturer where series ratings or selective coordination are relied on).
25. Check trim/door requirements against spec: door-in-door above the spec threshold (commonly 400 A), hinged trim, lockable doors, keyed-alike cylinders, and whether the spec requires a main breaker on every panel regardless of 408.36 exceptions.
26. Check SPD against the one-line and 26 43 13: present where shown, kA per mode, MCOV, Type 1 or Type 2, SCCR of the SPD matching the panel, integral vs. sidecar, and lead length limits.
27. Check submittal deviations: every variance from spec or drawing must be listed on the transmittal as a deviation or substitution request; a deviation found only in the body is grounds for "revise and resubmit."

## 3. Coordination with other trades

28. Switchboard/distribution gear supplier (26 24 13): send the list of panel feeder breakers; need back upstream breaker catalog numbers and trip settings so series combinations and coordination can be verified; settle before gear release (gear is the long-lead item, typically 4-6 months ahead of rough-in).
29. Short-circuit/coordination/arc-flash study engineer (26 05 73): send panel AIC/SCCR and breaker list; need back available fault current at each panel bus and confirmation that selective coordination holds where required (emergency, legally required standby, elevator); settle before panel release.
30. Mechanical (23 xx) and plumbing (22 xx): send mechanical panel schedules; need back MCA/MOCP from approved equipment submittals (RTUs, VFDs, water heaters, kitchen exhaust fans); settle before panel release or breakers get swapped in the field.
31. Food service equipment (11 40 00): send kitchen panel schedule; need back the equipment electrical schedule (voltage, amps, receptacle configuration, which circuits shunt-trip on hood suppression); settle before kitchen panel release and before hood/suppression rough-in.
32. Fire alarm (28 31 00): send the list of panels carrying fire alarm circuits; need back lock-on breaker requirement, red marking, and the shunt-trip/relay interface for hood and elevator; settle before fire alarm rough-in.
33. Elevator (14 20 00): send machine room panel data; need back controller MCA/MOCP, shunt-trip requirement where the hoistway/machine room is sprinklered, and the required lockable disconnect; settle before the elevator controller submittal is approved.
34. Architect/framing/finishes (09 21 16 gypsum, 06 41 00 casework): send flush-panel can dimensions and locations; need back stud depth, rated-wall box-out details, and casework clearances; settle before framing in each area.
35. Structural/seismic (26 05 48): send panel weights and mounting type; need back anchorage detail and whether certification is needed; settle before rough-in in the electrical rooms.
36. Owner/district facilities: send the proposed manufacturer and panel naming scheme; need back the district standard (most districts stock one manufacturer's breakers campus-wide) and keying preference; settle before submittal approval.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

37. Submittal panel schedule vs. drawing panel schedule: designation, voltage, bus amps, main device, space count, each circuit's trip and poles, load descriptions.
38. Submittal vs. one-line diagram: designation, bus amps, main device, SCCR/AIC, series vs. fully rated, "fed from" source, SPD presence, feed-through/sub-feed arrangement.
39. Submittal lug data vs. feeder schedule: conductor size, quantity per phase, CU vs. AL.
40. Submittal AIC/SCCR vs. short-circuit study: AIC at each panel exceeds the study's available fault current; any series combination matches the upstream device the study assumed.
41. Submittal dimensions vs. electrical room layouts and architectural wall sections: can size, mounting, working clearance, door swing, dedicated space above.
42. Submittal nameplate schedule vs. one-line: designation and "fed from" text match character for character (the one-line governs).
43. Submittal breaker types/trips vs. approved mechanical, kitchen, and elevator submittals: trip equals MOCP, HACR/shunt-trip types match the equipment interface.
44. Panelboard submittal vs. switchboard submittal: same manufacturer where single-source is specified; the switchboard feeder breaker appears in the panel's series rating table; SPD data matches the 26 43 13 submittal.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

45. Does each panel's SCCR/AIC exceed the available fault current at that location, and is the available fault current marked (110.9, 110.10, 110.24)? Answer from the study (research); the rule itself from memory.
46. Where series ratings are used, is the upstream device the exact tested combination and will the field marking per 110.22(B)/(C) and 240.86 be provided? Research the manufacturer's UL series table; the rule from memory.
47. Does the panel require a main overcurrent device, or does it qualify for a 408.36 exception (feeder OCPD not exceeding bus rating)? From memory; the old 42-overcurrent-device limit was dropped in NEC 2008, so 84-circuit panels are fine if listed.
48. Is working space (30" wide, 36"/42"/48" deep by condition at 151-600 V, 6.5 ft headroom) and the dedicated equipment space above the panel (110.26(E)) clear of ducts and piping? From memory; verify against the mechanical coordination model.
49. Is any panel in a prohibited location (clothes closet, over steps, near easily ignitable material per 240.24) or in a space the state school authority requires to be locked and inaccessible to students? NEC from memory; state DOE/school facilities authority rules by research.
50. Are GFCI breakers required for the circuits in question under the adopted edition's 210.8(B) (kitchens, outdoors, rooftop, within 6 ft of sinks, 250 V receptacles in 2020/2023)? Rule from memory; confirm the adopted edition and amendments with the local building department.
51. Is AFCI required anywhere in this K-12 (210.12 covers dwelling units, dormitories, hotel guest rooms, nursing home patient sleeping rooms; generally not classrooms)? From memory; check state amendments by research.
52. Is an SPD required at emergency/legally required standby panels (700.8, 701.? in 2020+) or at the service (230.67 for dwellings/dormitories/guest rooms, extended in 2023)? From memory; confirm adopted edition.
53. Does selective coordination apply (700.32, 701.32, 620.62 elevator feeders, 708.54 COPS) and does the study demonstrate it for the breakers submitted? Rule from memory; demonstration by research in the study.
54. Who provides arc-flash labels (110.16(B) at service equipment; NFPA 70E for the rest) and are they part of this submittal or the study submittal? From the spec; rule from memory.
55. Does the adopted energy code require disaggregated metering (IECC C405.12 over 25,000 sf, ASHRAE 90.1 8.4.3) that puts branch circuit monitoring in these panels? From memory; confirm adopted energy code with the AHJ.
56. Does the project's seismic design category and Ip require certification of the panelboard (ASCE 7 Ch. 13, Ip = 1.5 for life safety/essential), not just anchorage? Rule from memory; SDS and Ip from the structural general notes.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

57. Switchboard value-engineered to a different manufacturer after panels were approved; series-rated panels now have no tested upstream combination; AHJ rejects, main breakers or whole panels replaced at closeout.
58. Flush cans ordered standard depth for a 6" stud wall that was framed at 3-5/8", or recessed into a rated corridor wall with no box-out; panels protrude or the wall rating is voided; re-framing or surface-mount with ugly finish.
59. Copper bus specified, aluminum shipped because the cut sheet showed both and no one checked the order code; owner discovers it at closeout; credit or replacement interiors.
60. Breakers ordered from the drawing schedule before mechanical and kitchen equipment were approved; MOCPs differ; field breaker swaps, frame mismatches, spares consumed, and breakers of another brand installed in the panel (listing problem).
61. Main lugs sized for the standard feeder, not the actual one; installer uses pin adapters or splices in the gutter; 312.8 fill and bending-space violations.
62. Factory nameplates engraved with the sub's shop names ("LP-1A") instead of the drawing names ("L1A"); every directory, as-built, and the arc-flash labels are now inconsistent; re-engraving and real safety confusion for maintenance staff.
63. Top-feed panel installed where feeders stub up from below (or vice versa); conductors run the full gutter, bending space at the lugs fails 312.6.
64. Ductwork or piping routed through the dedicated space above the panel after rough-in; inspection fails, duct or panel relocated.
65. Panel released before the short-circuit study; panels near the transformer need 22 kAIC or higher, 10 kAIC panels delivered; series-rating hunt or replacement.
66. Rooftop or exterior panel shipped NEMA 1 or GFCI/AFCI breakers installed outside their temperature rating; water ingress, nuisance trips, replacement.

## 7. Industry standards to verify product claims against

67. UL 67 — Standard for Panelboards.
68. UL 489 — Molded-Case Circuit Breakers, Molded-Case Switches, and Circuit-Breaker Enclosures.
69. UL 50 / UL 50E — Enclosures for Electrical Equipment, Non-Environmental / Environmental Considerations.
70. NEMA PB 1 — Panelboards; NEMA PB 1.1 — General Instructions for Proper Installation, Operation, and Maintenance of Panelboards Rated 600 Volts or Less.
71. NEMA AB 1 — Molded-Case Circuit Breakers, Molded-Case Switches, and Circuit-Breaker Enclosures.
72. NEMA 250 — Enclosures for Electrical Equipment (1000 Volts Maximum).
73. UL 1449 — Surge Protective Devices (integral or sidecar SPDs).
74. UL 943 — Ground-Fault Circuit-Interrupters; UL 1053 — Ground-Fault Sensing and Relaying Equipment (ground-fault protection on mains).
75. NFPA 70 (NEC) adopted edition, NFPA 70E for arc-flash labeling, ASCE 7 Chapter 13 / ICC-ES AC156 for seismic certification, IEEE 1584 and IEEE 242 as the basis of the arc-flash and coordination studies the AIC values come from.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

76. K-12: panels in locked electrical rooms or closets, not corridors or classrooms; doors locked and keyed alike to the district standard; district-standard manufacturer so the maintenance shop stocks breakers; typed directories often required to use district room numbers, not the architect's; delivery must hit the summer shutdown window; GFCI at science labs, art rooms, kitchen, and exterior; NEMA 3R feeders for portable classrooms; lighting panels paired with relay panels, so wall space is coordinated for both.
77. Healthcare, essential electrical system (NEC 517, NFPA 99): separate panels for Life Safety, Critical, and Equipment branches, each labeled and color-identified, never mixed on one bus; Critical branch panels serving patient-care spaces require the redundant ground path of 517.13 (metal raceway plus insulated EGC) and the submittal must show a ground bar with enough lugs; selective coordination per 517.31(G) (0.1 s allowed) plus 700.32 for Life Safety; SPD on essential panels; second-level ground-fault protection under 517.17 can push mains to electronic-trip with GFP, which changes the frame and the panel depth.
78. Healthcare, patient-care details: operating rooms and wet procedure locations use isolated power panels with line isolation monitors (a separate section and submittal, but their feeder breakers and panel locations show up here); imaging and large mechanical loads push panel bus and breaker frames after equipment submittals, so hold panel release until medical equipment MOCPs are in; California HCAI/OSHPD requires OSP-preapproved seismic certification of the panelboard, not just anchorage, and panels in clean/sterile areas are flush with sealed trim.
