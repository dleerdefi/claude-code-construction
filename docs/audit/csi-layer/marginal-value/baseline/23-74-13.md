# Submittal Review Checklist — 23 74 13 Packaged Outdoor Central-Station Air-Handling Units (Rooftop Units)

Baseline project: K-12 school, gas/electric or heat-pump RTUs on curbs, BAS-integrated. Healthcare deltas noted in section 8.

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. One line per scheduled tag (RTU-1, RTU-2 ...) mapped to a full manufacturer model/configuration string, with the string decoded (cabinet size, heat type and size, voltage, options) — not a generic catalog cut.
2. Cooling performance run at the *scheduled* conditions: entering DB/WB, ambient DB, supply CFM, and outdoor-air CFM, showing total and sensible capacity, compressor kW, and the AHRI-certified IEER/EER (or SEER2/EER2 for units under 65 MBH).
3. Fan selection printout or curve for each fan (supply, powered exhaust/relief, return) showing the operating point, RPM, BHP, motor HP, and an itemized static pressure breakdown: external static as scheduled plus internal losses (dirty filter allowance, coils, economizer, heat exchanger, any ERV wheel).
4. Heating performance: gas input and output MBH, stages or modulation turndown, altitude derate applied if site elevation is above 2,000 ft; electric heat kW, stages, and whether SCR; heat-pump heating capacity at 47°F and 17°F with defrost and auxiliary heat lockout points.
5. Electrical data sheet: voltage/phase, MCA, MOCP, compressor RLA/LRA per circuit, fan motor FLA, electric heat amps, single- vs dual-point connection, factory disconnect (fused or non-fused), control panel SCCR in kA, and convenience outlet circuiting (unit-powered vs separate field circuit).
6. Dimensional drawing: overall L/W/H, operating and shipping weight, corner weights and center of gravity, lifting lug locations, service clearance envelope (condenser coil, filter pull, compressor/control compartment doors), and supply/return discharge arrangement (downflow vs horizontal) with opening sizes.
7. Roof curb drawing: curb height, gauge, insulation, wood nailer, pitched vs level (and roof slope assumed), duct support rails, gasket, knockout locations for power, controls, gas, and condensate; statement of seismic or wind rating if required.
8. Wiring diagrams: factory wiring, a field-wiring diagram with terminal strip designations, and a ladder showing safeties (high/low pressure, freezestat, high-limit, condensate overflow, phase monitor, smoke detector shutdown).
9. Controls submittal for the unit controller: physical point list (AI/AO/DI/DO), network object list, protocol (BACnet MS/TP or IP) with BTL listing claim, and a table of which functions the factory controller owns versus the BAS (economizer, DCV, morning warm-up, demand limit, occupancy, smoke mode).
10. Economizer data: control type (differential enthalpy, dry-bulb with high-limit), damper leakage class with AMCA 511 certification, actuator type, minimum OA position method (fixed, CO2 reset, or measured airflow), and a fault detection and diagnostics (FDD) compliance statement.
11. Filter data: size, count, depth, MERV rating per ASHRAE 52.2, pre/final arrangement, rack style (slide-out side load vs front load), and initial/final pressure drop at scheduled CFM.
12. Refrigerant: type (R-454B / R-32 A2L vs legacy R-410A), charge per circuit, number of circuits, and for A2L units the leak-detection sensor, mitigation sequence (fan run, compressor lockout), and UL 60335-2-40 listing.
13. Sound data: octave-band sound power at discharge, return/inlet, and casing-radiated, rated per AHRI 370 (outdoor) or AHRI 260 (ducted), at the submitted fan RPM.
14. Accessory list with yes/no per tag: hail guards, hinged access doors, service lights/GFCI, condensate overflow switch, phase/voltage monitor, low-ambient kit, hot-gas reheat or dehumidification mode, powered exhaust, CO2 sensor, smoke detectors (supply and return) and who furnishes them.
15. Warranty letter: compressor (5 yr typical), gas heat exchanger (10–15 yr), parts, and extended labor if specified; the factory start-up requirement that conditions the warranty.
16. Seismic/wind certification (where SDS and Ip require it): manufacturer's special certification (shake-table or analysis per ICC-ES AC156) covering the submitted model and curb anchorage calc.
17. Manufacturer's start-up checklist, IOM, and a statement of factory-authorized start-up service included.

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

18. Equipment schedule (M-6xx series) vs performance run: compare capacity at the *scheduled* entering/ambient conditions; reject runs printed only at AHRI standard 95°F / 80°F-67°F when the schedule uses a different design day.
19. Schedule sensible capacity vs submitted sensible: total often passes while sensible falls short on high-OA school units — this is the most common hidden rejection.
20. Schedule CFM and ESP vs fan operating point: confirm the submitted ESP is external only and the manufacturer added internal losses on top; confirm the motor HP is non-overloading across the curve, not just at the design point.
21. Schedule heating output (not input) vs submittal; electric heat kW and stages; heat-pump balance-point and auxiliary heat vs the designer's sizing note.
22. Electrical panel schedules and one-line (E-6xx, E-5xx) vs submitted MCA/MOCP/voltage: feeder ampacity at or above MCA, breaker at or below MOCP and consistent with the breaker type shown, voltage/phase matches the panel.
23. Available fault current on the electrical drawings vs unit control panel SCCR.
24. Structural roof framing plan and general notes (S-series) vs submitted operating weight, corner loads, and footprint: unit must sit on the framed opening shown and not exceed the structural engineer's assumed weight.
25. Mechanical roof plan and duct risers vs curb drawing: supply/return opening locations, discharge direction, and that the roof opening on the structural/architectural plans matches the curb inside dimensions.
26. Architectural roof plan, elevations, and screen wall details vs unit height on curb: confirm units are hidden behind screens/parapets as drawn and that screen clearance meets the manufacturer's condenser airflow clearance.
27. Spec Part 2 refrigerant and efficiency paragraphs vs submittal: many specs still name R-410A while production units are A2L; flag the mismatch for the engineer rather than approving silently.
28. Spec 23 40 00 / schedule filter MERV and depth vs submitted rack: confirm a 4-in MERV-13 fits, not a 2-in rack with a MERV-13 label and excessive pressure drop.
29. Spec 23 09 23 sequence of operations and points list vs the unit controller's object list: every sequence function (economizer high-limit type, DCV, morning warm-up, demand limiting, smoke mode, unoccupied setback) must map to an available point or factory function.
30. Spec acoustic criteria (or ANSI S12.60 for classrooms) vs sound power data: check the discharge and casing-radiated bands against the spaces directly below.
31. Plumbing drawings (P-series) vs condensate drain size/location and required trap depth for the pan static; gas riser vs gas connection size, inlet pressure range, and whether a regulator is needed.
32. Division 01 substitution procedures and addenda vs manufacturer: confirm the submitted make is named or was approved by prior-approval addendum.
33. Fire alarm drawings (FA-series) vs duct smoke detector scope: compare which detectors are shown, their locations (supply, return, or both), and who is listed to furnish them.
34. Structural general notes (SDS, Ip, wind speed) vs the seismic/wind certification submitted.
35. LEED/energy compliance form (COMcheck or equivalent) vs submitted IEER/EER and economizer/FDD claims.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

36. Electrical (26 05 00 / 26 24 16): send MCA, MOCP, voltage, FLA, disconnect responsibility, and convenience outlet circuit; need back feeder/breaker confirmation and whether the outlet needs a dedicated circuit; settle before electrical rough-in and before the panelboard submittal is approved.
37. Structural engineer (via RFI) and steel/joist supplier (05 12 00 / 05 21 00): send operating weight, corner loads, footprint, and curb dimensions; need back framing adequacy confirmation and final roof opening dimensions; settle before joist/steel shop drawings are released.
38. Roofing (07 50 00 series): send curb height, nailer, insulation, and flashing detail; need back the roofing manufacturer's minimum curb height above finished roof (8 in typical, 12 in in snow regions) and flashing compatibility; settle before roofing starts.
39. Controls (23 09 00 / 23 09 23): send unit controller model, protocol, and point list; need back a BACnet integration confirmation, sensor furnish/install responsibility matrix (CO2, DAT, space sensors), and economizer control ownership; settle before the controls submittal is approved and before the units are released to production.
40. Fire alarm (28 31 00): send duct detector arrangement and unit shutdown relay contacts; need back detector model, remote test station locations, and wiring; settle before the unit ships if factory-mounted detectors are used, otherwise before duct installation.
41. Plumbing (22 10 00 / 22 11 23 gas): send condensate drain location, size, and trap depth; gas connection size, location, and pressure range; need back drain routing and regulator/drip leg design; settle before roof penetrations are cored.
42. Sheet metal (23 31 13): send supply/return opening sizes, discharge direction, curb rail layout, and duct liner/flex connector requirements; need back duct shop drawings for the risers; settle before duct fabrication.
43. Test and balance (23 05 93): send fan curves, ESP, OA setpoints, and sheave type (fixed vs adjustable, or direct-drive ECM); need back a pre-construction flag if any fan is marginal; settle before unit release.
44. Crane/rigging (01 54 00): send weight, center of gravity, lifting lug locations, and curb set sequence; need back pick plan and crane location; settle before the roof deck is closed if units set after parapets.
45. Architect: send unit height, finish color, and screen wall clearance; need back screen height confirmation; settle before the screen wall submittal is approved.
46. Commissioning (01 91 13): send approved submittal and start-up checklist; need back pre-functional checklists and functional test scripts; settle before start-up is scheduled.
47. Lightning protection (26 41 13) if present: send unit locations for bonding; settle before roofing.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

48. Equipment schedule tag ↔ submittal cover ↔ dimensional drawing ↔ wiring diagram ↔ curb drawing: identical model/configuration string on every sheet.
49. Performance run ↔ fan curve: CFM, ESP, RPM, BHP, motor HP.
50. Performance run ↔ electrical data sheet: compressor count and size, fan motor HP, electric heat kW; those in turn ↔ MCA/MOCP on the electrical drawings.
51. Dimensional drawing ↔ curb drawing: base rail dimensions, supply/return opening locations, and corner-weight locations.
52. Dimensional drawing ↔ structural roof plan: operating weight and footprint location.
53. Wiring diagram ↔ controls point list: terminal designations for every field point and sensor.
54. Warranty letter ↔ spec Part 1 warranty paragraph: durations and labor coverage.
55. Shipping weight and CG ↔ rigging plan.
56. At delivery: unit nameplate (model, voltage, MCA/MOCP, refrigerant, charge) ↔ the approved submittal — reject a swapped substitution at the roof, not after start-up.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

57. Which IECC / ASHRAE 90.1 edition has the state or local AHJ adopted, and does it require economizers, FDD, and fan power limits for this unit size in this climate zone? (building department/energy code official — research the adopted edition and local amendments)
58. Does the submitted IEER/EER meet the DOE federal minimum for this capacity class (10 CFR 431)? (federal — research the current tier)
59. Are duct smoke detectors required on supply over 2,000 CFM per IMC 606.2 and NFPA 72, and does the fire marshal require return detectors as well? (fire marshal — answer from memory, confirm local amendments)
60. Does the DCV strategy still deliver ASHRAE 62.1 minimum OA for classroom occupancy at all stages, including the economizer minimum position? (mechanical code official — answer from memory)
61. Does the adopted mechanical code and UL 60335-2-40 edition allow the A2L refrigerant charge in this unit, and has the AHJ accepted A2L equipment? (building/mechanical official — research; jurisdictions lag)
62. Is the unit designated an Ip = 1.5 component under ASCE 7 Chapter 13 for this school (often an emergency shelter), triggering special certification? (structural engineer/AHJ — research structural general notes)
63. NEC 440.14 disconnect within sight, NEC 210.63 receptacle within 25 ft, NEC 110.10 SCCR vs available fault current, NEC 110.26 working clearance at the control panel — all satisfied? (electrical inspector — answer from memory)
64. Does the gas vent termination meet IFGC clearances from air intakes and roof edges, and is the gas pressure within the unit's listed range? (mechanical inspector — answer from memory)
65. Does the condensate drain meet IMC 307 (secondary drain or overflow switch, trap, cleanouts)? (mechanical inspector — answer from memory)
66. Does the state education authority (e.g., DSA in California, TDLR/TEA in Texas) impose its own ventilation, filtration, or acoustic requirements for school HVAC? (state agency — research)
67. Is there a property-line sound ordinance that governs condenser fan noise at night? (municipal — research)

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

68. Fan selected at the schedule's ESP without internal losses added; TAB cannot reach CFM, requiring sheave or motor changes after start-up and sometimes a different fan.
69. MOCP on the final unit exceeds the breaker shown on the panel schedule because an accessory (powered exhaust, electric heat) was added late; feeder and breaker change order after conduit is in.
70. Curb shipped before the duct layout was finalized, or set rotated 180°; supply/return land on the wrong side of the roof opening and roofing is already flashed.
71. Curb height set without accounting for tapered insulation or overlay; curb ends up below the minimum and the roofing warranty is voided, or units pond.
72. Condensate trap too shallow for the negative pan static; the pan floods, the overflow trips the unit, or water runs into the roof deck and classroom ceiling below.
73. Economizer dampers not low-leak or FDD not enabled; fails the energy code inspection and the unit runs with uncontrolled OA in winter.
74. Smoke detector scope gap: mechanical assumed fire alarm would furnish, fire alarm assumed factory-mounted; units ship without detectors, fire alarm acceptance fails.
75. Factory controller and BAS both believe they own the economizer and OA minimum; the two fight, the point list in the submittal never matched the sequence.
76. Phase monitor omitted on 3-phase units; compressor fails on single-phasing during a utility event and the warranty claim is contested.
77. Refrigerant spec mismatch: units arrive with A2L refrigerant the AHJ or the owner's service contract did not anticipate; leak sensors unsupported by the BAS points.
78. Center of gravity not on the submittal; crane pick is unbalanced and the unit base rail is bent on the set.
79. MERV-13 filters forced into a 2-in rack; pressure drop eats the fan margin and filters collapse into the coil.
80. Gas inlet pressure above the unit's listed maximum without a regulator; gas valve damaged and start-up aborted.
81. Start-up performed without duct connected or without permanent power, or by someone other than the factory-authorized technician; warranty void and the commissioning retest costs a mobilization.
82. Convenience outlet wired from the unit's own circuit; service tech cuts power at the disconnect and loses the outlet, or the inspector rejects it.
83. Hail guards omitted in a hail region; condenser coils flattened within a season.

84. AHRI 340/360 — Performance Rating of Commercial and Industrial Unitary Air-Conditioning and Heat Pump Equipment (IEER, 65 MBH and larger).
85. AHRI 210/240 — Performance Rating of Unitary Air-Conditioning and Air-Source Heat Pump Equipment (SEER2/EER2/HSPF2, under 65 MBH).
86. AHRI 370 — Sound Performance Rating of Large Air-Cooled Outdoor Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Equipment.
87. AHRI 260 — Sound Rating of Ducted Air Moving and Conditioning Equipment.
88. AHRI 1060 — Performance Rating of Air-to-Air Exchangers for Energy Recovery Ventilation Equipment (if an ERV wheel is included).
89. ASHRAE 90.1 / IECC — Energy Standard for Buildings Except Low-Rise Residential (economizer, FDD, fan power, efficiency).
90. ASHRAE 62.1 — Ventilation for Acceptable Indoor Air Quality.
91. ASHRAE 52.2 — Method of Testing General Ventilation Air-Cleaning Devices for Removal Efficiency by Particle Size (MERV).
92. ASHRAE 15 and ASHRAE 34 — Safety Standard for Refrigeration Systems; Designation and Safety Classification of Refrigerants.
93. UL 1995 / UL 60335-2-40 — Heating and Cooling Equipment safety listing (the latter required for A2L refrigerant units).
94. ANSI Z21.47 / CSA 2.3 — Gas-Fired Central Furnaces (gas heat section listing).
95. AMCA 210 — Laboratory Methods of Testing Fans for Certified Aerodynamic Performance Rating.
96. AMCA 500-D and AMCA 511 — Laboratory Methods of Testing Dampers for Rating; Certified Ratings Program for Air Control Devices (damper leakage class).
97. ASHRAE 135 (BACnet) and BTL listing — A Data Communication Protocol for Building Automation and Control Networks.
98. ANSI/ASA S12.60 — Acoustical Performance Criteria, Design Requirements, and Guidelines for Schools.
99. ASCE 7 Chapter 13 and ICC-ES AC156 — Seismic Design Requirements for Nonstructural Components; Seismic Certification by Shake-Table Testing.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

102. K-12 baseline: demand-controlled ventilation with CO2 sensors is routine for classrooms and gyms — confirm the DCV points and the economizer minimum-position reset; verify classroom sound criteria (ANSI S12.60, 35 dBA) against discharge sound power; expect MERV-13 as the owner's post-pandemic standard; check summer unoccupied dehumidification mode (hot-gas reheat) for closed-up buildings; confirm hail guards, lockable hinged doors, and screen walls for vandal and visual control; confirm morning warm-up and schedule-based occupancy in the sequence.
103. Healthcare (hospital, ASC, outpatient): review against ASHRAE 170 / FGI Guidelines instead of 62.1 alone — space pressurization and air change rates drive the fan selection, so check that the unit can hold constant volume against filter loading (airflow measurement or VFD tracking) rather than just meeting a design CFM.
104. Healthcare filtration: MERV-8 pre-filter and MERV-14 final filter minimum for patient areas, with the final filter downstream of the fan and coils; confirm the rack depth, gauge locations, and that the fan curve includes the final-filter dirty pressure drop.
105. Healthcare cabinet: double-wall construction with no exposed insulation in the airstream, sloped stainless drain pans with IAPMO-compliant drain, cabinet leakage rated per ASHRAE 193, and humidification or dehumidification capacity to hold 30–60% RH — none of these are standard on a school RTU and usually push the selection to a custom or semi-custom unit.
106. Healthcare power and redundancy: confirm which units serve critical spaces and are on the essential electrical system (NFPA 99 / NEC 517), and whether the engineer requires dual compressors/circuits or N+1 fan arrays for continuous operation.
107. Healthcare controls: economizers may be prohibited or limited for ORs and protective environments; smoke control and pressurization sequences tie into the fire alarm and must be reviewed with the life-safety drawings; infection control (ICRA) may require HEPA-filtered negative-pressure isolation during construction near existing air intakes.
108. Healthcare AHJ: expect state Department of Health review plus a Joint Commission-style commissioning record; the submittal should carry the ASHRAE 170 compliance statement and the air change calculations the designer used so the state reviewer can trace them.
