# 28 46 00 Fire Detection and Alarm — Submittal Review Checklist (K-12 baseline; healthcare notes in §8)

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Designer qualification on the cover: NICET Level III/IV in Fire Alarm Systems or PE stamp as the spec/AHJ requires, plus the installing contractor's state fire alarm license number.
2. Code summary sheet: occupancy (Group E), construction type, sprinklered yes/no, adopted editions of IBC/IFC and NFPA 72 (the edition the AHJ adopted, not the latest), and the evacuation concept (total vs. relocation).
3. Scaled floor plans on the contract architectural backgrounds showing every device with address, candela, mounting height, circuit ID (SLC/NAC/IDC), and conduit/cable routing.
4. Riser diagram: FACP, annunciator(s), every SLC loop with isolator positions, every NAC and booster power supply, EOL devices, all monitor/control modules (sprinkler, elevator, hood, duct detectors, door hardware, HVAC), and the communicator with both transmission paths.
5. Device schedule / BOM: quantities, manufacturer and model, UL category for each, with the panel manufacturer's compatibility listing showing every detector/base/module is listed with that FACP.
6. Battery calculations per FACP and per booster: standby current for 24 h plus alarm current for 5 min (15 min for voice systems), 20% derating, selected battery Ah and model, and proof it fits the cabinet.
7. NAC voltage-drop calculations for every circuit: starting voltage at end-of-battery (20.4 V for a 24 V system), wire gauge and length, device current at that voltage, end-of-line voltage above the lowest device minimum; method stated (lump-sum worst-case or point-to-point).
8. SLC loop calculations: addresses per loop vs. panel capacity, loop resistance and capacitance vs. manufacturer limits, Class A/X isolator placement if specified.
9. Sequence of operations as an input/output matrix: each initiating device type across the top, each output down the side (general alarm, voice message, strobes, HVAC shutdown, elevator recall/shunt, door holder release, access control unlock, PA mute, DACT transmission, supervisory, trouble).
10. Visible notification design: candela selection per room keyed to actual room size and ceiling height, corridor spacing, synchronization method across NACs sharing a field of view.
11. Audible/voice design: speaker layout with tap settings, target dBA (15 dBA over ambient, 5 dBA over max 60 s ambient), intelligibility approach for gym/cafeteria/auditorium, and the pre-recorded message scripts.
12. Pathway survivability level (0–3) stated for voice risers and interconnections between panels, with the cable or enclosure method that achieves it.
13. Supervising station details: UL-listed central station certificate (or proprietary station), communicator type (DACT/IP/cellular), and the account/line provisioning responsibility.
14. Mounting details: pull stations 42–48 in. to operable part, strobe lens 80–96 in. AFF, speaker/strobe combo, ceiling detector spacing on the actual ceiling construction, duct detector sampling tube orientation, exposed EMT in gyms.
15. Wiring method and cable types (FPL/FPLR/FPLP, CI cable where survivability requires), red-painted/labelled boxes and covers, firestop system references for rated penetrations.
16. Interface schedule listing every point monitored or controlled from another trade, by device, with who furnishes and who wires each side.
17. Deviation/substitution list against the spec and the district's standard manufacturer, and a statement of spare capacity (SLC addresses, NAC amps, cabinet space).
18. Acceptance testing plan, NFPA 72 Record of Completion template, and the owner training/as-built/password turnover package outline.

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

19. Lay the shop floor plans over the contract FA/E-series plans and confirm no device shown by the engineer was deleted; the contractor's design is additive, not a replacement.
20. Check system type (voice EVAC vs. horn/strobe) and class designations (SLC/NAC Class A, B, or X) against the spec paragraph and IBC 907.2.3 for Group E.
21. Check FACP manufacturer/model and open-vs-proprietary programming against the spec's listed manufacturers and the district standard; route any substitution through Division 01.
22. Pull the architectural RCPs and building sections and verify the ceiling height used in each strobe calc and the beam/joist pockets used for smoke detector spacing are the real ones (gym, cafeteria, stage, media center).
23. Check device selection by room: heat detectors in kitchens and dirty mechanical rooms, beam detectors in high gyms, no smoke detectors near cooking or shop dust unless the spec requires them.
24. Verify pull station count and location against the IBC 907.2.3 exception (sprinklered + voice = single station at a constantly attended location) and the spec; if the full set is required, check 200 ft travel and every exit door.
25. Compare the duct detector list to the mechanical AHU schedule: every supply > 2,000 cfm and return > 15,000 cfm serving more than one story (IMC 606), located and oriented as the mechanical drawings show them.
26. Verify a monitor module exists for every waterflow, tamper, PIV, backflow valve, fire pump controller signal, and pre-action/dry valve shown on the 21 13 00 shop drawings.
27. Check elevator interface: lobby, machine room, and hoistway detectors for primary/alternate recall, heat detectors for shunt trip where the machine room is sprinklered, and the fire hat output.
28. Check the HVAC shutdown and smoke damper outputs in the matrix against the 23 09 93 sequences of operation.
29. Check door holder, magnetic lock, delayed egress, and access-controlled egress releases against the 08 71 00 hardware schedule and IBC 1010.2.
30. Check wiring method against the spec and Division 26 raceway requirements; confirm plenum ratings where cable is in return-air ceilings.
31. Verify NAC and booster loading against the power supply rating with the specified spare margin (typically 20–25%) and that SLC address spare meets the spec.
32. Verify the communicator meets NFPA 72 Ch. 26 (two paths or single path with the allowed supervision interval) and that someone owns the phone lines/IP drop/cellular plan.
33. Check mounting heights and device coverage against ADA 2010 Standards (strobes in all public and common-use areas including restrooms) and ICC A117.1.
34. Check the annunciator and FACP locations against the fire department's known preferences (main entry, Knox box) and the architectural room names.
35. Confirm firestop details reference tested systems for the actual cable/conduit and wall assemblies.
36. Confirm the AHJ package is the same revision as the one submitted to the design team, is sealed as the AHJ requires, and shows the correct code editions.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

37. 21 13 00 Fire Suppression: send riser and monitor module list; need back sprinkler shop drawings with every flow/tamper/PIV/backflow/fire pump signal; settle before sprinkler riser and overhead mains are installed.
38. 23 00 00 HVAC / 23 09 00 Controls: send duct detector list and shutdown matrix; need back AHU schedule (cfm, locations), access door locations, and the shutdown interface method (FA relay to unit controller or BAS); settle before AHU submittals are approved and ductwork is hung.
39. 11 40 00 / 21 13 00 Kitchen hood suppression: need the suppression system microswitch type and location and whether it trips alarm or supervisory; settle before hood installation.
40. 14 20 00 Elevators: send recall/shunt sequence and detector locations; need back the controller's input requirements (primary, alternate, fire hat, shunt trip) and machine room location; settle before the elevator controller is released for manufacture.
41. 26 05 00 / 26 24 16 Electrical: dedicated branch circuits for FACP, boosters, and annunciators with red breaker lock-offs, raceway/sleeves, generator or emergency power source if specified; settle before panelboard schedules are finalized and wall rough-in.
42. 08 71 00 Door Hardware: send release matrix; need back the hardware schedule with holder/mag lock/delayed egress power supplies and fail-safe behavior; settle before hardware submittal approval.
43. 28 13 00 Access Control / 28 52 00 Lockdown: define alarm release vs. lockdown priority and which system wins; need back the access control panel interface; settle before head-end programming.
44. 27 51 00 PA/Intercom: define PA mute on alarm and whether voice EVAC is also the mass-notification path; settle before head-end equipment order.
45. 09 51 00 Ceilings / BIM coordination: device positions against lights, diffusers, sprinklers, and projectors in the RCP; settle in the overhead coordination model before ceiling grid.
46. 07 84 00 Firestopping: penetration schedule for FA cable/conduit through rated assemblies; settle before rough-in.
47. 26 32 13 Generator: running/fault/transfer supervisory signals to the FACP if required; settle before generator controller submittal.
48. Owner IT and telecom: IP communicator network drop, static IP, firewall rules, POTS or cellular service, monitoring contract; settle 60 days before scheduled acceptance test.
49. AHJ / Fire Marshal: stamped approval of the FA package before any rough-in; acceptance test scheduled with the fire department and the elevator inspector in the same window.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

50. Riser vs. floor plans: circuit IDs, device count per circuit, EOL device locations, isolator positions.
51. Floor plans vs. device schedule/BOM: quantities of each model.
52. NAC voltage-drop calcs vs. floor plans: wire length, device count, and the candela rating of each strobe (current depends on candela).
53. Battery calcs vs. product data sheets vs. device schedule: per-device standby and alarm currents and counts.
54. Candela on floor plans vs. strobe calc table vs. architectural room dimensions and RCP ceiling heights.
55. Sequence matrix vs. 23 09 93 HVAC sequences vs. 14 20 00 recall sequence vs. 08 71 00 hardware schedule.
56. Product data UL listings vs. FACP compatibility listing: compatibility identifiers for every detector, base, and module.
57. SLC loop calc vs. riser: address count per loop and isolator module locations.
58. Duct detector locations: FA plans vs. mechanical plans vs. AHU schedule.
59. Monitor module list vs. sprinkler shop drawings: one-to-one for every switch.
60. Pathway survivability on the riser vs. the cable product data (CI cable listing) or the rated enclosure detail.
61. Code summary sheet vs. calculation methods and tables used (NFPA 72 edition-specific candela/spacing tables).
62. FACP location on FA plans vs. electrical circuit on panelboard schedule vs. architectural room name.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

63. Which IBC/IFC and NFPA 72 editions and state amendments has the state fire marshal / local AHJ adopted, and does the state department of education impose school-specific standards? Research.
64. Does the Group E occupant load trigger voice EVAC under IBC 907.2.3, and which exceptions apply? Memory for the rule; verify occupant load from the code analysis.
65. Does the AHJ accept the single-pull-station exception, and does it require a PE seal or a specific NICET level on the drawings? Research with the AHJ.
66. Will the AHJ accept a single IP or cellular path under NFPA 72 Ch. 26, or require two paths? Memory for the code; research the AHJ's practice.
67. What pathway survivability level applies to the voice system (NFPA 72 Ch. 24: Level 2/3 for partial evacuation/relocation, Level 0/1 acceptable for total evacuation)? Memory.
68. Is CO detection required under IBC/IFC 915 for the school's fuel-fired appliances, and does the AHJ want it on the fire alarm panel? Memory for the rule; research the AHJ's position.
69. Which ASME A17.1 edition governs elevator recall and shunt trip, and does the state elevator inspector have supplemental requirements? Research.
70. Does the kitchen hood suppression trip need to initiate alarm per IFC 904 when a fire alarm exists? Memory.
71. Does NFPA 72 require a smoke detector at the FACP and at remote power supplies, and is the room sprinklered or constantly attended? Memory.
72. Does the lockdown/mass notification function require an NFPA 72 Ch. 24 risk analysis and AHJ sign-off on message priority? Memory for the requirement; research the AHJ's expectation.
73. Are strobes required in every classroom and restroom under ADA 2010 Standards 215/702 and IBC 907.5.2.3? Memory.
74. Which sprinkler control valves must be electrically supervised (IBC 903.4) given the backflow and PIV arrangement? Memory.
75. Does the AHJ require a UL central station certificate or accept a listed proprietary/remote station? Research.
76. Can FA conductors share raceway with other power-limited systems under NEC 760, and does the spec prohibit it anyway? Memory.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

77. Voltage drop run at 24 V nominal instead of 20.4 V end-of-battery; strobes at the end of long NACs drop out during the 24-hour battery test; rework or add booster.
78. Candela picked for a 10 ft ceiling in a 24 ft gym or cafeteria; coverage fails; strobes added after ceilings and bleachers are in.
79. Strobes on different NACs in the same corridor/gym not synchronized; AHJ rejects; sync modules retrofitted.
80. Duct detectors on FA plans but not on mechanical drawings; units arrive without access doors, sampling tubes face the wrong way, no shutdown wiring; ceiling opened after inspection.
81. Backflow preventer or added control valve shows up on the sprinkler shop drawings after FA approval; no monitor module; supervisory nuisance at acceptance.
82. Elevator controller expects four dry contacts, FA provides two; shunt trip missing; elevator inspection fails and occupancy slips.
83. Battery calc counts alarm current on only one NAC; batteries undersized; 24 h + 5 min test fails and batteries replaced.
84. Smoke detectors in gym, shop, or near kitchen; nuisance alarms; staff disables zones and the system is out of service during school hours.
85. Spare capacity consumed by field changes; no NAC headroom for the strobes the AHJ asks for at walkthrough.
86. POTS lines not ordered or district firewall blocks the IP communicator; monitoring not active; no certificate of occupancy.
87. Voice intelligibility fails in gym/cafeteria (hard surfaces); speakers re-tapped or added, often after acoustical ceilings are closed.
88. Voice riser run in ordinary FPLP where Level 2 survivability was required; whole riser repulled in CI cable.
89. Mag locks wired to release on alarm but the access control power supply is fail-secure; egress doors stay locked on alarm; failed life safety inspection.
90. Lockdown PA announcement and fire alarm voice message both play; no mute priority defined; confusion at drills.
91. Device addresses and panel labels do not match as-builts; fire department cannot locate the alarm; annunciator relabelled at turnover.
92. FA cable penetrations through rated corridor walls not firestopped because cable was pulled after the wall inspection; fire marshal walks it at final.
93. Detector heads installed before painting and ceiling work; contaminated heads replaced at contractor cost.
94. Programming matrix never finalized; HVAC does not shut down at the acceptance test; controls contractor and FA contractor point at each other.
95. AHJ requires a PE seal the submittal lacks; package rejected and rough-in held.
96. Turnover missing record of completion, programming passwords, and software; district cannot make changes and must pay the installer.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

108. K-12: Group E voice EVAC, single-pull-station exception, pull station tamper covers with local sounders against pranks, PA mute, and lockdown/mass-notification priority all have to be settled in the matrix.
109. K-12: gym, cafeteria, auditorium, and stage drive the intelligibility design and high-ceiling detection; kitchen hood and CO (IBC 915) interfaces are the usual misses.
110. K-12: district standard manufacturer, monitoring vendor, and programming access are owner requirements that override "or equal."
111. Healthcare: CMS enforces NFPA 101 (2012) and NFPA 99 (2012) on top of the state code, so the review runs against two editions; confirm which governs each item.
112. Healthcare: defend-in-place means relocation/partial evacuation, so voice pathways require Level 2/3 survivability (2-hour CI cable or rated enclosures), and notification is zoned by smoke compartment.
113. Healthcare: private-mode notification in patient care areas (visible only, or staff-only audible), nurse call and medical gas alarm interfaces, corridor door holders released by compartment, smoke damper and smoke door release per compartment.
114. Healthcare: FACP and boosters on the life safety branch of the essential electrical system (NEC 517), multiple elevator groups, fire pump and generator supervision.
115. Healthcare: phased work in an occupied facility requires interim life safety measures, ICRA controls for above-ceiling work, and a tie-in plan for the existing panel network that the submittal must show.
