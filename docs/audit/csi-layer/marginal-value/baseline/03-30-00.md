# 03 30 00 Cast-in-Place Concrete — Submittal Review Checklist (PE baseline, closed-book)

Assumed project: mid-rise office or K-12 school, spread footings / SOG / elevated composite or flat-plate slabs / shear walls / site flatwork.

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. One mix design per concrete class and placement condition on the structural concrete schedule (footings, SOG, elevated slabs, walls, columns, topping, site flatwork, grout pads, pump vs. chute versions), each showing: f'c and test age, max w/cm, cementitious content with SCM percentages, aggregate source and nominal max size, design slump or slump flow (with/without HRWR), air content and tolerance, unit weight, yield per cubic yard, and all admixtures with dosage ranges.
2. Strength basis for each mix per ACI 301/318: standard deviation from at least 15 (preferably 30) consecutive field tests from the same plant and similar materials within the last 24 months, or trial-batch data, and the resulting required average strength f'cr.
3. Aggregate test data: ASTM C33 gradation per source, specific gravity and absorption, soundness, LA abrasion, deleterious substances, and ASR evaluation (C1260/C1567 or C1293 results, or documented mitigation).
4. Mill certificates for cement (ASTM C150 type, or C595/C1157), fly ash (C618 Class F/C with LOI), slag (C989 grade), silica fume (C1240) — chemical and physical values, not just a cover letter.
5. Admixture product data with ASTM designation (C494 Type A/D/F/G, C260 AEA, C1017 plasticizer), dosage range, chloride content statement (non-chloride accelerator), and a single-manufacturer compatibility statement when more than one admixture is in a mix.
6. Water source: potable, or ASTM C1602 test results for reclaimed/wash water.
7. Reinforcing: placing drawings and bar lists/bending schedules in ACI 315 / CRSI format with bar marks, lap lengths and class, hook geometry, dowel projections, bar support types (CRSI class; plastic-tipped or stainless at exposed/architectural faces), mill certs (A615 vs. A706 by grade and heat), epoxy (A775) or galvanized (A767) certs where specified, mechanical splice data with ACI 318 Type 1/Type 2 classification.
8. WWR style designation per ASTM A1064, flat sheets only (no rolls), with support spacing.
9. Embed and anchor rod setting drawings: plan location dimensioned to grids, top-of-concrete elevation and projection, rod diameter/grade (F1554 Gr 36/55/105, with weldability supplement S1 where field welding is possible), nuts/washers/plate washers, templates, plate thickness and headed stud size/length (A108, welded per AWS D1.1 Clause 7), and the setting tolerance the detailer is committing to.
10. Curing compound data: ASTM C309 type and class or C1315, dissipating vs. non-dissipating, application rate, VOC, and written compatibility statement with the floor finish/adhesive system scheduled on top.
11. Vapor retarder data: ASTM E1745 class (A/B/C), thickness (15 mil typical; 10 mil minimum), permeance after conditioning, seam tape, pipe boots, and manufacturer installation instructions (ASTM E1643).
12. Miscellaneous products: evaporation retarder, form release agent, form liners, expansion joint filler (D1751/D1752), waterstops (PVC/hydrophilic), non-shrink grout (C1107), fibers (C1116 with dosage), bond breaker, dowel baskets/plate dowels, early-entry saw blade/joint sealant data.
13. Quality plan: ready-mix plant NRMCA certification, testing lab ASTM C1077 accreditation, ACI Field Technician Grade I cards, cylinder sampling point (chute vs. end of hose), initial curing box, test frequency, break schedule (7/28/56-day plus hold cylinders), and who reports to whom.
14. Hot-weather and cold-weather concreting plans (ACI 305R/306R) matching the pour season: max concrete temperature, retarder/accelerator use, haul time, protection and heating method, monitoring.
15. Placement, finishing and joint plans: pour sequence drawing, control/construction joint layout for every SOG and elevated slab, saw-cut timing and depth, finishing method per area, FF/FL numbers and who measures per ASTM E1155.
16. Shoring/reshoring and formwork drawings for elevated slabs stamped by an engineer (ACI 347R; OSHA 1926.703), with reshore removal strength criteria tied to field-cured cylinder breaks.

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

17. Element-by-element: compare each mix's f'c, test age, max w/cm, air content, and max aggregate size against the structural general notes concrete schedule and the 03 30 00 mix table; flag any element with no mix and any mix with no element.
18. Exposure class check (ACI 318 Table 19.3.1.1 / 19.3.2.1): exterior flatwork and foundations in freeze-thaw zones need F2/F3 (air 5–7%, w/cm ≤0.45), sulfate class from the geotech report, W1/W2 for below-grade walls — confirm the mix meets the most severe class, not the one the plant assumed.
19. Recompute f'cr: f'c + 1.34s and f'c + 2.33s − 500 (f'c ≤ 5000 psi), take the larger; if no standard deviation record, use the ACI 301 Table (f'c + 1200 for 3000–5000 psi) — reject mixes that only show average strength without a record.
20. Max aggregate size versus geometry: ≤1/5 narrowest form dimension, ≤1/3 slab depth, ≤3/4 clear bar spacing — check against the most congested element (columns, boundary elements, pile caps, beam-column joints), not the typical slab.
21. SCM limits: fly ash ≤25%, slag ≤50%, combined ≤50% (ACI 318 Table 26.4.2.2(b) for F3) unless the SER allows more; separately judge finishability — high SCM in a cold-weather trowel-finished slab is a finishing problem the spec table will not catch.
22. Interior hard-trowel slabs must NOT be air-entrained (target ≤3%); exterior flatwork must be; check the mix table does not reuse one mix for both.
23. Chloride ion content: sum admixture, aggregate and water chlorides against the Cl limit for the element's exposure class (C1 interior 0.30% by weight of cement, C2 0.15%, prestressed 0.06%).
24. Slump: verify the design slump is stated at point of placement, that pump mixes carry a separate slump/air target, and that HRWR-adjusted slump is identified so the inspector does not reject a legitimate 8" slump.
25. Placing drawings against structural plans and schedules: bar size, spacing, top vs. bottom placement, lap lengths and class (Class B default), hook dimensions, column tie/hoop spacing and 135° seismic hooks, corner and intersection bars at walls, extra bars at openings, dowels into masonry/steel, and all typical details (step footings, pilasters, slab edges, beam-column joints).
26. Bar grade: A706 wherever bars are welded or are part of the seismic-force-resisting system (ACI 318 Ch 18); check A615 Gr 80 is not substituted where the drawings say Gr 60.
27. Cover and supports: 3" cast against earth, 2" formed and exposed to weather (#6+), 1-1/2" (#5 and smaller), 3/4" interior slab/wall — then check the selected bolster/chair heights actually deliver that cover for the thickest bar bundle, not the nominal slab.
28. Anchor rod drawings against the steel column schedule and base plate details (05 12 00) and mechanical equipment pad drawings: diameter, grade, pattern, projection above top of concrete, orientation relative to grid, embedment, and whether the SER called for anchor reinforcement or oversized holes.
29. Embed plates against the connecting trade's details: elevation referenced to top-of-slab/wall (never top-of-form), plate size, stud count/length relative to slab thickness, orientation marks, and whether each embed is on the structural drawings or only on another trade's shop drawings (then route for SER acceptance).
30. Vapor retarder: directly beneath the slab (no sand blotter unless specifically detailed per ACI 302.2R), lapped and taped, penetrations booted, turned up at perimeter; compare thickness/class against both 03 30 00 and the flooring spec's moisture requirement.
31. Curing by element: wet cure duration for slabs receiving flooring vs. compound elsewhere; reject non-dissipating compound anywhere flooring, sealers, or waterproofing must bond unless the flooring manufacturer has approved it in writing.
32. Test frequency vs. spec/ACI 318 26.12: one set per 150 cy, per 5,000 sf of slab/wall, per day per class, minimum 5 tests per class; confirm cylinder size (4x8 vs. 6x12) and that the lab uses C31 initial curing boxes.
33. Joint layout against structural notes and ACI 302.1R: control joint spacing ≈ 24–36 × slab thickness (inches → feet), aspect ratio ≤1.5:1, cut depth ≥1/4 slab thickness (early-entry 1"), construction joints only where the SER permits, isolation joints at columns/walls, joints aligned with column lines and reentrant corners.
34. SOG against the geotechnical report: subgrade modulus assumed, base course thickness/gradation, moisture conditioning, and whether the geotech requires a capillary break under the vapor retarder.
35. Lightweight concrete on composite deck: equilibrium density (C567) and air limits, fire-rating thickness over deck (UL design), and the 05 31 00 deck pour-stop and shear stud sequence.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

36. Structural steel (05 12 00): send anchor rod and embed setting drawings; need back the base plate schedule/erection drawings with rod pattern, projection and plate-washer requirements confirmed, and any grade substitution; settle before foundation rebar fabrication and no later than first footing pour.
37. Metal deck and composite studs (05 31 00): send slab pour sequence and mix (NW vs. LW); need back deck pour-stop/closure details and stud welding schedule; settle before first elevated deck pour.
38. Geotechnical engineer / soils inspector (31 23 00, 01 45 00): send SOG base and vapor retarder sequence; need back subgrade acceptance protocol and proof-roll/density requirements; settle before base course placement.
39. Plumbing/mechanical/electrical (22, 23, 26): send slab plans with joint layout and conduit rules (conduit ≤1/3 slab thickness, ≥3 diameters apart, not crossing in the same plane, no conduit in topping); need back a coordinated under-slab utility and in-slab conduit layout, floor drain elevations/slopes, and housekeeping pad schedule with anchor bolt locations; settle before vapor retarder is placed.
40. Fire protection (21 13 00) and MEP hangers: send elevated slab rebar layout; need back cast-in insert/hanger layout; settle before deck rebar is placed each floor.
41. Elevator (14 20 00): send pit and hoistway drawings; need back rail bracket inserts, pit ladder/sump locations, divider beam pockets, hoistway plumb tolerance; settle before pit slab and hoistway wall pours.
42. Masonry (04 20 00) and precast (03 45 00): send foundation and slab edge plans; need back dowel sizes/spacings, brick ledge elevations, precast connection embed locations; settle before foundation wall rebar fabrication.
43. Curtain wall/storefront (08 44 13): send slab edge and embed layout; need back edge embed locations and the slab-edge tolerance they require (often tighter than ACI 117 ±1"); settle before each floor's edge forms are set.
44. Waterproofing (07 10 00) and below-grade insulation: send wall forming and tie plan; need back waterstop type/location, allowable form-tie type and patching method, required surface finish and cure compound restriction for membrane bond; settle before foundation wall forming.
45. Flooring (09 60 00 / 09 30 00) and polished concrete (03 35 43): send SOG mix (w/cm), curing method and joint layout; need back moisture limits (ASTM F2170 RH), FF/FL minimums, compatibility sign-off on curing compound, and joint pattern acceptance for exposed floors; settle before the SOG mix design is approved, because w/cm drives drying time.
46. Site civil (32 13 13): send exterior flatwork mix and joint plan; need back curb/walk sections and ADA slopes; settle before site concrete pours.
47. Testing agency / Special Inspector (01 45 00): send the full approved package; need back their inspection plan, cylinder curing box location, sampling point agreement, and break reporting path; settle before the pre-pour meeting for the first placement.
48. Post-tensioning (03 38 00) if present: tendon profiles, pour strips, stressing strength and sequence; settle before first deck forming.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

49. Mix design sheet ↔ batch ticket (sample the first delivery): mix ID, cementitious weights, SCM percentages, design w/cm, admixture products and dosages, design slump/air.
50. Mix design ↔ structural concrete schedule: f'c, element, exposure class, max w/cm, air, max aggregate size, test age.
51. Mix design ↔ mill certificates: cement type and plant/source, SCM class and source named on the mix are the same ones on the certs.
52. Mix design ↔ admixture data sheets: product names, ASTM types, dosage within the published range, same manufacturer family.
53. Placing drawings ↔ bar list: bar marks, quantities, lengths, bend types, grade; and ↔ structural plans: size, spacing, location, lap length, hook type.
54. Rebar mill certs ↔ bar list/delivery tags: grade, size, heat numbers; coating certs ↔ coated bars on list.
55. Anchor rod setting drawing ↔ structural base plate schedule ↔ steel erection drawings: rod diameter, grade, pattern, projection, embedment, plate size and hole size.
56. Embed schedule ↔ connecting trade shop drawings: plate size, stud count/length, elevation, orientation; embed count per floor matches.
57. Joint layout plan ↔ structural notes/details (joint type, spacing, allowed construction joint locations) ↔ architectural floor pattern and polished-concrete layout.
58. Curing compound data ↔ flooring manufacturer requirements; vapor retarder data ↔ slab detail and flooring moisture spec.
59. QC/testing plan ↔ Statement of Special Inspections and 01 45 00 frequencies; hot/cold-weather plan ↔ admixtures actually on the mix sheet ↔ pour schedule.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

60. Which ACI 318 exposure class (F, S, W, C) governs each element and what w/cm, f'c, air and chloride limits does it impose? — SER's general notes; I answer the ACI table from memory and check the project assignment.
61. Is the sulfate class supported by an actual soil/groundwater sulfate test in the geotech report, or was S0 assumed? — geotechnical engineer; research the report.
62. What is the seismic design category, and does it force A706 bar and Chapter 18 detailing for walls/frames? — SER drawings; from the drawings.
63. Which concrete special inspections and frequencies does IBC Chapter 17 (Table 1705.3) and the Statement of Special Inspections require for this project? — building official / SER; research the project statement.
64. Is the aggregate source on the state DOT's reactive aggregate list, and does ASTM C1778 require mitigation? — state DOT / SER; research.
65. Does a state facility authority (e.g., California DSA for schools, HCAI/OSHPD for healthcare) impose its own testing, inspection, and mix approval requirements beyond IBC? — state agency; research.
66. Is a licensed engineer's stamp required on shoring/reshoring and formwork drawings by OSHA 1926.703 or state rule? — OSHA/state; OSHA from memory, state rule research.
67. Does the local air district (e.g., SCAQMD) or LEED/CHPS requirement cap curing compound and sealer VOC? — air district; research.
68. Is radon-resistant sub-slab construction required by the local amendment (IBC Appendix F / state radon map)? — AHJ; research.
69. Does ACI 318 26.12 minimum testing (and 26.12.6 for low-strength investigation) match what the spec requires, and who pays for cores? — spec/ACI; from memory.
70. Are the testing lab and special inspector approved by the building official and ASTM C1077 accredited? — AHJ; research.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

71. Rebar fabricated from unreviewed or superseded placing drawings — wrong laps, hooks, or dowel projections; result: field cutting, SER RFIs, lap-length deficiencies caught at inspection, pour delayed.
72. Anchor rods set from a template built off the setting drawing without checking the steel shop drawings — rod pattern rotated or projection short; result: slotted base plates, field-welded washers, post-installed anchors with engineer letters.
73. Mix submitted by the plant as a generic mix with unsupported f'cr — low 28-day breaks; result: cores, strength investigation, possible load test or remediation.
74. Air-entrained mix delivered to an interior trowel-finished slab — blisters and delamination after occupancy; result: grind or replace.
75. Water added at the truck beyond the single permitted adjustment (ASTM C94) — w/cm exceeded; result: low strength, scaling, high RH that fails flooring moisture tests.
76. Vapor retarder punctured by chairs and foot traffic, seams untaped, boots missing — floor adhesive failure and moisture claims years later.
77. Non-dissipating curing compound sprayed where flooring/epoxy must bond, or compound applied below rate — bond failure, or plastic shrinkage cracking and curling.
78. Saw cuts made too late in hot weather or too shallow — random cracking across the panel; result: patching, owner rejection on exposed floors.
79. Embeds and inserts floated or kicked during vibration — steel connections and curtain wall brackets don't land; result: post-installed anchors, SER review of reduced capacity.
80. Conduits bundled in slabs over 1/3 the slab depth or stacked at column strips — cracking along the run and SER rejection.
81. Hot weather with no retarder and long haul times — cold joints and plastic shrinkage cracks; cold weather with no protection — frozen or scaled surface, early strength loss, reshores removed on optimistic breaks.
82. Field cylinders left in a truck bed or on the deck instead of a curing box — artificially low breaks; result: disputed results, cores, schedule hit.
83. Top bars walked down in slabs, chairs missing or too short — top steel ends up mid-depth; result: cracking at columns, capacity questions on cantilevers.
84. Air loss through the pump (1–2%) not accounted for on exterior flatwork — scaling after first winter.
85. FF/FL not measured within 72 hours of the pour — tolerance disputes with no defensible data; grinding at the contractor's cost.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

94. K-12 school: state school-facility agency (e.g., DSA) may require its own mix approval, inspector-of-record sign-off, and higher sampling frequency; exposed/polished corridor slabs need one aggregate source and plant for color consistency and an architect-approved joint pattern; gym slabs need tighter FF/FL for sprung wood floors; kitchen slabs need depressed/sloped areas and drain coordination; playground and canopy flatwork is F3 exposure.
95. Mid-rise office: lightweight concrete on composite deck (equilibrium density, UL fire thickness, longer drying for carpet/resilient floors); slab-edge tolerance driven by curtain wall; raised-access-floor pedestal adhesive compatibility with curing compound; vibration criteria on long-span floors.
96. Healthcare: HCAI/OSHPD-type oversight; high-density concrete for imaging shielding with density verification; non-ferrous reinforcing or shielding details at MRI; tighter vibration-sensitive floor criteria near surgical/imaging suites.
97. Warehouse/industrial: superflat (F-min) tolerances for VNA racking, plate or basket dowels at every joint, armored joints under forklift traffic, higher fiber dosage or shrinkage-compensating mixes, joint layout tied to racking plan.
98. Parking structures: C2/F3 exposure, w/cm ≤0.40, corrosion inhibitor and epoxy-coated or galvanized bar, sealer and drainage slopes, post-tensioning interface.
99. Water-holding / wastewater: ACI 350 crack-control reinforcing, waterstops at all joints, w/cm ≤0.45, hydrostatic leak test before backfill.
100. Labs/data centers/cleanrooms: low-shrinkage mixes, vibration criteria, raised floor loading, no curing compound where epoxy coatings follow.
