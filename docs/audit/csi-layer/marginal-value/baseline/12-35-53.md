# 12 35 53 Laboratory Casework — Submittal Review Checklist

Baseline: K-12 science lab (student stations with sinks, teacher demo table, prep/chem storage room, one demonstration fume hood). Healthcare and research variations noted in section 8.

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Casework plans at 1/4" = 1'-0" minimum with every unit tagged by a unit number that also appears on the elevations, the unit schedule, and the fixture schedule.
2. Elevations of every run at 1/2" = 1'-0" showing unit widths, filler/scribe widths, counter height, backsplash height, wall cabinet bottom and top heights, and sink/fixture locations.
3. Large-scale (3" = 1'-0") sections through base cabinet, wall cabinet, wall-hung counter support, pipe chase/service turret, sink cabinet, and fume hood base.
4. Service rough-in drawing: plan with dimensioned coordinates (from column lines or finished walls) for every supply, waste, vent, gas, vacuum/air, and electrical stub-up or wall penetration, with elevations of each.
5. Unit schedule listing model number, nominal size, door/drawer configuration, shelf count, lock yes/no, and material/finish for each unit.
6. Service fixture schedule with manufacturer model number per fixture type (hot/cold mixing faucet, cold-only, gas cock, cup sink, vacuum/air turret), outlet type (serrated nozzle, aerator), handle color code, vacuum breaker model, and mounting (deck/turret).
7. Sink schedule: bowl size and depth, material (molded epoxy, polypropylene, stainless), drop-in vs undermount, outlet/strainer/tailpiece size, and the trap material called out (polypropylene mechanical-joint for chemical waste).
8. Work-surface layout drawing showing top material, thickness, seam locations, edge profile (marine/drip edge at sinks), cutouts, and backsplash/end splash lengths.
9. Hardware cut sheets: hinges, pulls, drawer slides (load rating), shelf clips, locks (keying system named), catches.
10. Finish samples: two 12" x 12" minimum door/drawer-front samples per color (metal or wood), 6" x 6" top samples per material/color, laminate chips, hardware samples in specified finish.
11. Fastening and anchoring details to wall, floor, and ceiling, including anchor type, embedment, spacing, and required in-wall blocking; stamped seismic calculations where the project is in a jurisdiction that requires them (CA DSA, high-seismic SDC).
12. Hazardous (flammable, corrosive) storage cabinet cut sheets showing listing mark (UL 1275 or FM), capacity in gallons, door type (manual or self-closing), vent bung treatment, and lining material for acid cabinets.
13. Fume hood base cabinet data: width matched to hood, vented or unvented, exhaust collar size/location if vented, and whether the base is "acid" or "flammable" type.
14. Manufacturer test reports showing the casework line meets SEFA 8 (M, W, or PL as applicable) structural/durability tests and tops meet SEFA 3 chemical resistance.
15. Composite-wood core certification (CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI, NAUF if specified) and VOC emission certification (CDPH v1.2 / GREENGUARD Gold) when schools-grade indoor air quality is specified.
16. Installer qualification statement and the manufacturer's SEFA 2 installation instructions.
17. Warranty statement with term and coverage (typical: 1 year labor, lifetime or 10-year on casework structure, 5 years tops).
18. Keying schedule or statement that locks will be keyed per the owner's building master system (coordinated with 08 71 00).

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

19. Compare each unit on the shop drawing elevations against the architect's interior elevations (A-series, usually A-6xx or A-5xx) for unit count, widths, door swings, and total run length; fillers shall not exceed the spec maximum (typically 3" to 6").
20. Compare counter heights to the drawings and ADA stations: standing stations 36", seated 30"–32" (middle school), accessible 34" max rim, and children's 26"–30" where the architect has designated them.
21. Verify material against Part 2 of the spec: metal (gauge, powder-coat chemical-resistant finish), wood (species, cut, finish system), or plastic laminate (NEMA LD 3 grade, core); confirm no substitution of core (particleboard vs plywood vs MDF).
22. Verify top material and thickness called in the spec (typically 1" molded epoxy, black; sometimes phenolic or solid surface at K-12 non-chemistry rooms) and that drip grooves/marine edges are shown where the spec requires them at sink runs.
23. Confirm each service fixture matches the plumbing fixture schedule tag (P-xx) and the spec's listed manufacturer; confirm every faucet with a serrated hose outlet has an integral or separate ASSE 1035/1001 vacuum breaker.
24. Confirm sink material and size match the plumbing drawings and that bowl location in the top clears the cabinet stiles and the P-trap clears the back of the cabinet and the drawer above.
25. Check the rough-in drawing against the plumbing (P-1xx) and electrical (E-1xx) plans: every stub-up on the casework drawing has a matching device on the MEP drawings and vice versa.
26. Check that the hood base cabinet width equals the fume hood width shown in 11 53 13 and on the mechanical drawings, and that the hood's cup sink and fixtures are not double-counted between 11 53 13 and 12 35 53.
27. Confirm the demo table includes the teacher master controls called out on the drawings (gas emergency shutoff, electrical emergency shutoff, water shutoff) and that their locations are shown.
28. Verify lock locations against the drawings' lock symbols or the spec's blanket rule (e.g., "all doors and drawers in prep rooms locked").
29. Verify hazardous storage cabinet quantities, capacities, and types (flammable vs corrosive vs separate oxidizer) match the drawings' equipment schedule and the owner's chemical inventory if one was issued.
30. Check tall cabinet and wall cabinet heights against ceiling heights on the RCP and the 18" sprinkler deflector rule; confirm soffits or closures above tall units are shown where the drawings call them.
31. Check that wall-hung counters show a support system (steel C-frames, cantilever brackets, or aprons) with a stated load rating meeting the spec (SEFA 8 base unit load of 200 lb per lineal foot is a common target) and show the in-wall blocking or structure they depend on.
32. Check drawer slide load rating, shelf thickness/span, and adjustable shelf hardware against the spec grade (AWI custom/premium or SEFA 8).
33. Confirm the submittal identifies all "by others" items (final fixture connections, traps, electrical devices in raceways, eyewash) so the scope gap is visible and resolvable.
34. Confirm accessible stations show 27" high x 30" wide x 19" deep knee/toe clearance with no drawer or apron intrusion, and show pipe protection at the sink.
35. Check glazing in glazed doors is tempered or laminated safety glazing (16 CFR 1201 Cat I) where glass is within 18" of the floor or in doors.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

36. 22 13 16 / 22 66 53 (sanitary and chemical waste) and 03 30 00 (slab): send the dimensioned casework rough-in drawing; get back the plumber's underslab coordination drawing with matching coordinates; settled before underslab rough-in inspection and slab pour. This is the single most time-critical coordination for this section.
37. 22 11 16 / 22 42 00 (domestic water and plumbing fixtures): send the fixture schedule; get back written confirmation of who furnishes and who installs faucets, sinks, traps, vacuum breakers, and tailpieces, and the stub-out heights; settled before plumbing fixture submittal approval and wall rough-in.
38. 23 11 23 (facility natural gas) and 22 11 23: send gas cock count, demand, and demo table master valve location; get back pipe routing in the chase and the emergency shutoff valve model/location; settled before gas rough-in.
39. 26 05 33 / 26 27 26 (electrical raceways and devices): send device locations in backsplashes, pedestals, and casework raceways plus emergency shutoff button location; get back feed points, box sizes, GFCI designations, and the UL listing path for raceways built into casework; settled before wall rough-in.
40. 06 10 00 rough carpentry / 09 22 16 non-structural framing: send a blocking drawing with heights and extents for wall cabinets, wall-hung counters, tall cabinet anchors, and eyewash; get back confirmation of installed blocking (photo or inspection) before drywall closes.
41. 11 53 13 laboratory fume hoods and 23 31 00 ductwork: send hood base width/type and venting requirement; get back hood exhaust collar location and duct drop to confirm clearance over tall units; settled before above-ceiling duct rough-in.
42. 21 13 13 fire sprinkler: send tall cabinet heights and locations; get back head placement that keeps 18" clearance and avoids heads directly over fixed tall units; settled before sprinkler shop drawing approval.
43. 09 65 00 resilient flooring / 09 65 13 base: send casework sequence request; get back agreement on flooring under casework vs around toe kicks, and cove vs straight base at toe kicks; settled before flooring installation starts.
44. 09 91 23 painting: send the install sequence; get back confirmation that walls behind and above casework are finish-painted before casework arrives; settled at the finish schedule meeting.
45. 08 71 00 door hardware: send the casework lock product data; get back the keying schedule and cylinder format compatibility; settled before casework lock cylinders are ordered (typically 8 weeks before delivery).
46. 10 44 00 / 22 45 00 emergency eyewash and shower: send location of deck-mounted eyewash in the demo top or sink; get back the fixture model, tempered water (ASSE 1070) supply requirement, and drain routing; settled with the plumbing rough-in.
47. 27 15 00 / 27 41 00 data and AV: send teacher station outlet and grommet locations; get back box/pathway requirements; settled before wall rough-in.
48. Owner FF&E and equipment: send the openings and clearances provided (refrigerator, dishwasher, undercounter equipment); get back final equipment cut sheets; settled before shop drawing approval.
49. 08 51 00 windows / 08 80 00 glazing: send counter and backsplash heights at exterior walls; get back sill heights and operable sash hardware clearances; settled before shop drawing approval.
50. 07 92 00 sealants: confirm who seals tops to walls and the sealant type (chemical-resistant silicone or epoxy caulk); settled before top installation.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

51. Casework rough-in drawing vs plumbing coordination drawing: X/Y coordinates of each stub-up, pipe size, and material within 1/2".
52. Casework fixture schedule vs plumbing fixture schedule (P-drawings): fixture tag, model number, outlet type, vacuum breaker, and furnished-by/installed-by columns.
53. Casework elevations vs electrical device plans: receptacle and switch mounting heights and horizontal locations relative to unit edges.
54. Fume hood submittal (11 53 13) vs casework hood base: width, depth, venting, cup sink presence, and fixture quantities.
55. Casework anchor details vs structural/seismic documents: anchor type, spacing, and the wall/floor substrate assumed in the calculation.
56. Shop drawing unit widths vs architect's interior elevations: run totals, filler widths, door swing hand, and unit tags.
57. Casework sample submittal vs the architect's finish/color schedule: color name and manufacturer for metal, laminate, wood stain, and top.
58. Casework lock schedule vs 08 71 00 keying schedule: key-alike groups by room and master key hierarchy.
59. Hazardous storage cabinet schedule vs code-analysis sheet (G-series) and owner chemical inventory: capacity per cabinet and total per control area.
60. Reflected ceiling plan vs tall unit heights: clearance above tall units and soffit/closure extents.
61. Spec Part 2 product list vs cut-sheet model numbers: basis of design and any substitution request on file.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

62. Is anchorage of casework required by the AHJ, and with what documentation (ASCE 7 Ch. 13 component anchorage; CA DSA for K-12, HCAI/OSHPD for healthcare)? Research the project's SDC, Ip, and the local amendments; do not answer from memory.
63. Are self-closing doors required on flammable storage cabinets in this jurisdiction (NFPA 30 allows manual or self-closing; many fire marshals and state fire codes mandate self-closing)? Research with the fire marshal.
64. May the flammable cabinet be vented, and if so how (NFPA 30: not required; if vented, to exterior with flame arrestor, otherwise bungs capped)? From memory, confirm with the fire marshal if the drawings show a vent.
65. What is the maximum allowable quantity of flammable liquids in a Group E occupancy per control area, and does the lab count as its own control area (IFC Ch. 50/57 and Table 5003.1.1(1), NFPA 45 Ch. 12 for educational labs)? Research the adopted edition; this sets how many cabinets are allowed.
66. How many accessible student stations and sinks are required and at what height (ADA 2010 §902 and §606; children's dimensions where applicable; state education code)? Memory for ADA numbers; research the state education agency rule.
67. Is a vacuum breaker on every hose-end lab faucet sufficient, or does the plumbing code/water purveyor require a reduced-pressure backflow assembly on the lab water branch? Memory for the faucet device (ASSE 1035); research the local cross-connection control rule.
68. Is acid neutralization or dilution required before chemical waste discharges to sanitary (local sewer authority, plumbing code)? Research the sewer use ordinance.
69. Is a teacher-controlled master gas shutoff with key or emergency valve required (IFGC, NFPA 54, state education safety guidelines)? Research the state requirement; most states require it in school science rooms.
70. Are GFCI receptacles required at all lab counter receptacles within 6 ft of a sink (NEC 210.8(B))? From memory: yes in non-dwelling occupancies; confirm the adopted NEC edition.
71. Is an emergency eyewash required within 10 seconds of travel from any hazardous-chemical use point and does it need tempered water (ANSI/ISEA Z358.1, OSHA 1910.151(c))? From memory; location is a design-team decision but the casework must accommodate it.
72. Does the composite wood in the casework meet the federal formaldehyde emission rule (TSCA Title VI, 40 CFR 770) and does the school district require GREENGUARD/CHPS low-emitting furniture? Memory for TSCA; research the district's requirement.
73. Do tall cabinets maintain 18" clearance below sprinkler deflectors and avoid creating shielded areas (NFPA 13)? From memory.
74. Are aisle widths between fixed casework at least 36" (IBC 1018 aisles; NFPA 45 and NSTA recommend 44"+ in instructional labs) and does the lab need two exits? Memory for IBC; research NFPA 45 and the state education code for the specific lab.
75. Is a corrosive/acid storage cabinet required to be separated from flammables and from bases, and does the AHJ require a listed cabinet or accept a lined wood/metal casework unit? Research with the fire marshal and the owner's chemical hygiene plan.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

76. Underslab plumbing is roughed before casework shop drawings are approved, so stub-ups land outside the sink cabinet; consequence: slab saw-cutting, repiping, schedule delay, and cabinet modification.
77. Blocking is omitted or installed at the wrong height for wall-hung counters and wall cabinets; consequence: drywall is opened or surface-mounted plates are used, and counters sag or pull loose under student loading.
78. Epoxy tops arrive and are installed in unconditioned space or field-cut without planning; consequence: hairline cracks, stress fractures at sink corners, and visible seams at the demo station.
79. Marine (raised) edge omitted at sink runs; consequence: water runs onto the floor and into cabinet joints, swelling wood or laminate cores.
80. Sink cutout does not match the submitted bowl size, or the P-trap collides with the drawer above; consequence: field-cut cabinets and non-compliant trap arms.
81. Faucet model changed at procurement and arrives without the vacuum breaker; consequence: plumbing final inspection fails and faucets are replaced.
82. Chemical waste traps installed in PVC rather than polypropylene to match the acid-waste piping; consequence: incompatible joints, leaks, replacement.
83. Receptacle cutouts in metal backsplashes do not align with wall boxes; consequence: extension rings, exposed box edges, or a second backsplash.
84. Fillers and scribes too narrow for out-of-plumb walls; consequence: visible gaps, shim stacks, failed punch list.
85. Casework delivered before HVAC is operating; consequence: wood warps, doors go out of alignment, finish checks.
86. Flammable cabinets arrive non-listed, undersized, or with manual-close doors in a self-closing jurisdiction; consequence: fire marshal rejection at final.
87. Locks delivered with manufacturer's standard cylinders not compatible with the building master key; consequence: all cylinders replaced after occupancy or chemicals left in unlocked cabinets.
88. "By others" gap on final fixture connections between the casework installer and the plumber; consequence: fixtures sit unconnected at substantial completion.
89. Pipe chase in island/peninsula built without removable access panels; consequence: valves and traps are unreachable for maintenance.
90. Tall storage cabinet placed under a sprinkler head or hard against the ceiling without a closure; consequence: fire inspection correction and ceiling damage.
91. Accessible station provided at 34" but drawers or apron intrude into the knee space and supply/drain lines are uninsulated; consequence: ADA deficiency, rework.
92. Demo table gas cocks installed but the master shutoff valve location was never agreed; consequence: gas stays locked out and the room cannot be used as designed.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

116. K-12 (baseline): confirm the teacher demo station carries the master gas, electrical, and water shutoffs; rounded edges and no sharp exposed hardware; every chemical storage door lockable; plastic laminate acceptable in biology/earth science rooms but epoxy at chemistry and demo tops; GREENGUARD/CHPS low-emitting certification often mandatory; chemical quantities limited by Group E MAQ and NFPA 45 Ch. 12; state education agency may impose its own lab safety standards (e.g., Texas, California DSA).
117. Healthcare (clinical lab, pharmacy, procedure rooms): expect stainless steel or solid-surface tops with integral coved backsplashes, no particleboard cores, seamless cleanable finishes, hands-free hand-wash sink stations, and compliance with FGI Guidelines and NFPA 99; in California, HCAI/OSHPD requires pre-approved anchorage (OPM/OPA) and a stamped anchorage drawing; ICRA sequencing governs delivery into occupied areas; many of these units are properly 12 35 70 Healthcare Casework and the review must sort which section governs each unit.
118. Research/university lab: expect SEFA 10 adaptable or mobile tables with overhead or ceiling-hung service carriers, higher load ratings, vibration-isolated balance tables, DI/RO water in polypropylene with dedicated fixtures, lab gases (vacuum, compressed air, N2, CO2) with purge/pressure requirements, multiple fume hoods tested to ASHRAE 110, biosafety cabinets to NSF/ANSI 49 with clearances, sealed BSL-2 surfaces, chemical storage classified per NFPA 45 laboratory unit class, and a lab planner/EHS reviewer whose comments must be closed before approval.
119. Any facility with ductless hoods, vented chemical storage, or snorkel exhaust: the vent path from the cabinet or base to the exhaust system must be shown on both the casework and mechanical submittals, and the fire marshal's acceptance of venting obtained before fabrication.
