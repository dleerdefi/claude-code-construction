# 05 51 00 Metal Stairs — Submittal Review Checklist (closed-book baseline)

Assumed project: mid-rise office or K-12 school, steel or concrete frame, IBC-based jurisdiction. Reviewer: GC Project Engineer. Items marked [memory] I answer from experience; [research] I look up for the specific job.

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Shop drawings keyed to the architectural stair IDs (ST-1, ST-2, …), with a plan at every landing, a longitudinal section through every flight, and an elevation of every guard/handrail run — one package per stair, not a "typical" sheet.
2. Stringer member for each flight: shape and size (channel, MC, HSS, bent plate), ASTM grade, orientation (closed vs. open riser, stringer flush or exposed), and any field splice location.
3. Tread/riser construction: pan gauge and sheet grade, pan depth (fill thickness), riser closure, nosing type (abrasive cast, extruded, integral bent), and whether fill is by this trade or "by others".
4. Landing framing: headers, hangers/posts, deck type (checker plate vs. filled pan), and how the landing carries to structure (post to slab, hanger from beam above, or wall-supported).
5. A connection detail at every support point: top and bottom of each stringer, each landing post base, each hanger, each wall connection — with plate thickness, bolt diameter/grade/count, weld size/type/length, and slotted-hole slip allowances where required.
6. An anchor schedule: manufacturer and product, diameter, embedment, minimum edge distance and spacing, base material (normal-weight concrete, slab-on-deck, CMU), cracked/uncracked assumption, and the ICC-ES ESR number.
7. Guard and handrail details: post size/wall thickness and spacing, top rail, infill (pickets, mesh, panel) with clear opening dimension, handrail diameter and bracket type/spacing, wall bracket anchorage, extensions and returns at every top and bottom.
8. Delegated-design calculations sealed by a PE licensed in the project state, covering stringers, treads/pans, landings, every connection, guards, handrails and brackets, and anchors to concrete — not just stringers.
9. Design loads stated on the first calc page: 100 psf stair live load, 300 lb concentrated on 4 sq in, guard 50 plf and 200 lb concentrated, infill 50 lb on 1 sq ft, handrail 200 lb any direction, plus seismic component forces (Fp) if the stair is a nonstructural component.
10. A reaction table at every support, in a form the EOR can check against the building structure.
11. Field-verification statement: which dimensions were designed from drawings and which were field-measured (floor-to-floor, slab-edge location, wall-to-wall) with the survey date.
12. Shop finish: primer product, SSPC surface prep, DFT; hot-dip galvanizing for exterior/garage stairs; whether handrails are shipped loose or welded.
13. Product data for: abrasive nosings, grating treads (if used), checker plate, pipe/tube, bolts, post-installed anchors, shop primer, and any proprietary tread insert.
14. Welder qualification and WPS references (AWS D1.1 for structural, D1.3 for sheet pans); AISC fabricator certification if the spec calls for it.
15. Erection notes: pick points, temporary bracing, sequence relative to slab pours, and whether the stair is intended for construction access before fill and guards are in.
16. Clouded deviation list on the cover sheet; a submittal with no deviations noted but obvious substitutions (e.g., plate stringers for channels) goes back.

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

17. Recompute riser height and tread depth for every flight from structural top-of-slab elevations (S sheets) plus finish floor build-up (A finish schedule); riser 4" min / 7" max, tread 11" min, max 3/8" variation within a flight; check the top and bottom risers specifically.
18. Clear width between handrails and between walls against the A stair plans and the life-safety plan's egress capacity (44" min for occupant load ≥ 50; 36" below that).
19. Headroom 80" min along the nosing line, checked under landings above and under any beam, duct, or sprinkler main shown in the coordination model.
20. Landing dimensions: at least the stair width in the direction of travel (not required to exceed 48" for straight runs), and door swing at a landing not reducing it below half the required width.
21. Stringer size/grade against spec minimums and the structural general notes; confirm whether the spec allows bent-plate stringers or requires rolled channels.
22. Pan depth against the specified finish (tile, terrazzo, sealed concrete): the finish thickness plus setting bed must fit and still leave the nosing flush.
23. Guard height 42" min measured from the nosing line; handrail 34"–38"; both drawn and dimensioned on the elevation, not just noted.
24. Handrail graspability (1-1/4" to 2" OD circular, or a Type II shape with the perimeter limits), 1-1/2" clear to wall, bracket spacing within the calc span, 12" horizontal extension at top, one tread depth sloped extension at bottom, returns to wall or post.
25. Guard opening limitations: 4" sphere up to 36", 4-3/8" between 36" and 42", 6" sphere in the tread/riser/guard triangle; check these at fabrication tolerance, not nominal.
26. Nosing profile: projection 1-1/4" max, radius 9/16" max, closed risers in egress stairs; check that the nosing product data matches the drawn profile.
27. Scope line against 05 12 00: which support members (landing headers, edge angles, hangers) are on the S sheets and which are in this package; nothing can be in neither.
28. Bolt grades and weld symbols against the structural general notes (A325/F3125 for structural connections, E70XX electrodes, minimum weld sizes per AISC Table J2.4).
29. Finish against 09 91 00 and 05 05 13: primer compatibility with the field topcoat, and galvanizing where the spec or exposure requires it.
30. Stair construction in a rated enclosure: noncombustible materials only, no stringer or landing support penetrating a shaft wall without a detail.
31. Anchor locations against slab edges and pour-stop locations in the model: edge distance in the calc must be achievable on the actual slab.
32. Delegated engineer's license state and the calc design criteria (code edition, Seismic Design Category, Ip, ap/Rp) against the structural general notes.
33. Calc support assumptions against what the structure actually provides: a stringer assumed pinned on an edge angle needs that edge angle on the S sheets; a post assumed on a 6" slab can't be on a 3-1/4" slab-on-deck.
34. Guard post base connection and handrail bracket anchorage have their own calc pages with the 200 lb load — these are the most commonly missing items.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

35. 05 12 00 Structural Steel / EOR: send the stair reaction table and anchor/embed locations; need back EOR acceptance and any added embeds or edge angles; settled before structural steel shop drawings are released for the affected levels.
36. 03 30 00 Cast-in-Place Concrete: send embed plate and cast-in anchor locations per level; need back confirmation they're on the concrete placing drawings; settled before each slab pour (last chance — after that it's post-installed anchors and a redesign).
37. 03 30 00 / 03 35 00 Concrete Finishing: settle who places and finishes the pan fill, mesh in the pans, and the nosing flush condition; settled before stair erection.
38. 09 30 00 Tile / 09 66 00 Terrazzo: send pan depth and nosing detail; need back finish thickness and setting-bed thickness; settled before pan fabrication.
39. 05 52 13 Pipe Railings / 05 73 00 Decorative Railings / 06 40 00 Wood Handrail: settle the scope boundary (who furnishes guards at landings, floor-edge guards, wood cap), and post spacing/locations; settled before railing fabrication.
40. 08 11 13 Doors / 08 71 00 Hardware: send landing dimensions and handrail extensions; need back door swing and closer arm clearance; settled before stair layout approval (extensions regularly clash with door swings).
41. 09 21 16 Gypsum / Shaft Wall framing: send wall bracket locations and loads; need back blocking (plate or strap in metal studs) detail; settled before walls are closed.
42. 07 84 00 Firestopping: send every rated-wall penetration created by a stringer or landing support; need back a listed system; settled before rated wall inspection.
43. 23 31 00 Ductwork / 21 13 13 Sprinklers / 26 51 00 Lighting: run the stair soffit and landings through clash detection; need back routing that preserves 80" headroom and fixture/conduit mounting on stringers; settled before fabrication (stringer penetrations can't be field-cut without engineering).
44. 26 50 00 Lighting / 26 05 00: stair lighting and exit sign mounting on stair steel need pre-drilled holes or clips; settled before shop fabrication.
45. Survey/VDC: field-verify floor-to-floor and slab-edge location after the structure is topped out and before release to fabrication; settled before fabrication, which usually means the stair is on the critical path for slab-on-deck sequencing.
46. 09 91 00 Painting: settle primer system and whether rails are field-painted or powder-coated; settled before shop coating.
47. 10 14 00 Signage: stair ID and floor level signs mount on walls or stair steel; settle location before stringer fabrication if mounted to steel.
48. Special Inspection agency: post-installed anchors and field welds need inspection scheduling; settled before erection starts.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

49. Shop drawing floor-to-floor vs. S top-of-slab elevations plus A finish schedule thickness: the riser count × riser height must equal the verified dimension.
50. Calc member sizes vs. shop drawing member sizes and the bill of materials — revised shop drawings often outrun the calc.
51. Calc reactions vs. S drawing stair reaction allowances or the EOR's written acceptance.
52. Anchor calc (product, diameter, embedment, edge distance, f'c, cracked/uncracked) vs. shop drawing anchor schedule vs. ESR report vs. S general notes f'c.
53. Guard/handrail heights, post spacing, and picket spacing on shop drawings vs. the loading and span used in the calc vs. the A details.
54. Shop drawing finish notes vs. primer product data vs. the 09 91 00 paint schedule vs. the spec's galvanizing requirement.
55. Nosing/tread product data vs. the drawn tread detail vs. the A finish schedule (abrasive, contrast stripe if required).
56. Weld symbols and bolt grades on shop drawings vs. the WPS and welder certs vs. the S general notes.
57. Material ASTM designations on the BOM vs. the Fy assumed in the calc (A36 vs. A572 Gr 50 vs. A500 Gr B/C).
58. Spec 05 51 00 submittal article vs. the transmittal contents: calcs, certifications, product data, mockup if required, all present.
59. Coordination model geometry vs. shop drawing geometry: post locations, landing extents, stringer soffit elevation.
60. Stair hand (left/right) on shop drawings vs. A plans — the mirror-image stair is a real failure.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

74. Top or bottom riser off by more than 3/8" because slab elevation or finish thickness changed after fabrication — fails inspection, trip hazard, bottom flight rebuilt or landing shimmed.
75. Stair fabricated to drawing dimensions without field verification — flight doesn't land on the slab, landing post lands on a beam flange or a floor box.
76. Guard post base anchored too close to the slab edge or into slab-on-deck rib — concrete breakout, posts wobble, remediated with through-bolts and edge angles.
77. Handrail wall brackets on toggles in gypsum board with no blocking — bracket pulls out at the 200 lb load; walls opened to add plates.
78. Pan fill cracks or delaminates because no mesh, inadequate thickness, or fill placed before stair was plumb; nosing not cast flush so tile cracks at every tread.
79. Shop primer incompatible with field topcoat, or paint over unpassivated galvanizing — finish delaminates in the first year.
80. Handrail extensions omitted or clashing with door swing — cut off in the field, ADA inspection fails.
81. Picket spacing exceeds 4" after fabrication tolerance accumulates over a run — rework or add infill.
82. Headroom under a landing lost to a duct or sprinkler main added after stair approval — relocate utilities or rebuild landing.
83. Stair used for construction access before fill and permanent guards — bent pans, debris in pans, damaged welds, temporary guards never installed properly.
84. Field stringer splice or landing post cut to fit, not in the calc — unapproved modification, requires engineer's letter.
85. Rigid top-of-stair connection with no drift accommodation — connection tears or stringer buckles in a seismic event; a known post-earthquake failure.
86. Landing hung from a beam the EOR never saw a reaction for — beam web crippling or visible deflection.
87. Stair built mirror-image of the plan — scrapped flight.

92. ASTM A123/A123M — hot-dip galvanizing of products; ASTM A153/A153M — galvanizing of hardware; ASTM A780 — repair of galvanized coatings.
93. ASTM F3125/F3125M — high-strength structural bolts; ASTM A307 — carbon steel bolts.
94. ANSI/NAAMM MBG 531 — Metal Bar Grating Manual; NAAMM AMP 510 — Metal Stairs Manual; NAAMM AMP 521 — Pipe Railing Systems Manual.
95. ASCE/SEI 7 — Minimum Design Loads and Associated Criteria (Ch. 4 loads, Ch. 13 nonstructural components).
96. ACI 318 Ch. 17 — Anchoring to Concrete; ICC-ES AC193 (mechanical anchors) and AC308 (adhesive anchors) evaluation reports.
97. ICC A117.1 — Accessible and Usable Buildings and Facilities; 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design (§504 stairways, §505 handrails).

100. ASTM A1064/A1064M — welded wire reinforcement in pan fill.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

101. K-12 school (Group E): heavy, abusive traffic — demand abrasive cast nosings and closed risers; guards must be non-climbable (no horizontal intermediate rails, pickets only); California DSA review adds contrast stripes and its own approval cycle; check for ship's ladders to roof mechanical areas (OSHA, not IBC).
102. Mid-rise office: communicating/convenience stairs are often open with glass guards under 05 73 00 — scope boundary and 42" guard height on the open side; roof access stair required if the roof has equipment (IBC 1011.12).
103. High-rise (>75'): luminous egress markings (IBC 1025), stair pressurization affecting door hardware and landing size, and 56" width where stairs serve more than 2,000 occupants.
104. Healthcare (Group I-2): handrails both sides and continuous, closed risers, cleanable finishes; FGI Guidelines may add width and handrail requirements beyond IBC.
105. Residential (R-2): riser 7-3/4" / tread 10" permitted within units but egress stairs still at 7"/11"; guard 42".
106. Industrial / mezzanine access: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.25 governs non-egress stairs (ship stairs 50°–70°, tread 9-1/2" min, 200 lb/1,000 lb loading), not IBC — confirm which regime each stair is in.
107. Parking garages and exterior stairs: hot-dip galvanize everything, open grating or drained treads, snow and ice slip resistance, stainless or galvanized anchors; thin pans warp in the galvanizing bath — require heavier gauge or post-galvanize straightening.
108. Correctional (I-3): anti-ligature handrails, no climbable guards, tamper-proof fasteners, and the 4" sphere exception does not necessarily apply — read the facility standard.
109. Assembly (A occupancy): aisle stairs in seating areas follow IBC 1030, with different riser/tread and handrail rules — don't review them under 1011.
