# 07 54 00 Thermoplastic Membrane Roofing (TPO / PVC) — Submittal Review Checklist

Baseline project: new K-12 school, steel deck, fully adhered or mechanically attached single-ply over tapered polyiso with cover board, 20-year NDL warranty. Written closed-book from GC project-engineer experience.

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Membrane data sheet stating polymer (TPO, PVC, or PVC-KEE), ASTM designation and type, reinforcement (polyester scrim), nominal thickness (45/60/80 mil) and thickness over scrim, sheet widths offered, color, and CRRC-listed initial and 3-year aged solar reflectance and emittance.
2. One-page system component schedule naming manufacturer and product for every layer: vapor retarder (if any), insulation, tapered insulation, cover board, fasteners and plates (or adhesive/foam with coverage rate), membrane, bonding adhesive, seam cover strips, flashing membrane, termination bar, sealants, pourable sealer, pipe boots, walkway pads — all supplied or expressly accepted by the membrane manufacturer so the warranty covers the whole assembly.
3. Roof plan shop drawing showing every roof area, slope arrows, crickets and saddles with ridge/valley lines, drains, overflow drains/scuppers, curbs, penetrations, parapet heights, expansion joints, and wind zones (field / perimeter / corner) dimensioned.
4. Tapered insulation fabricator's layout: panel-by-panel plan with panel designations, number of layers, starting thickness, slope, thickness at every drain and high point, and a calculated average R-value for each roof area.
5. Securement drawing per wind zone: fastener and plate spacing (mechanically attached: fastener row spacing tied to sheet width; adhered: insulation fastening density and adhesive bead spacing / coverage) for field, perimeter and corner, keyed to the uplift calculation.
6. Wind uplift calculation (manufacturer or engineer, sealed if the spec requires) listing code edition, ASCE 7 edition, basic wind speed, exposure, risk category, mean roof height, enclosure classification, parapet height, and resulting design pressures per zone with the safety factor applied.
7. Specific tested-assembly listing (FM RoofNav assembly number or UL TGFU/TGIK listing) that matches the proposed deck, insulation type and thickness range, cover board, attachment, and membrane — not a generic "our products are FM approved" letter.
8. Fire classification evidence: UL 790 / ASTM E108 Class A listing for the deck type and the maximum slope on this roof, and UL 1256 or FM 4450 Class 1 listing where the spec or insurer requires it over steel deck.
9. Flashing details for every condition on this roof, either drawn project-specifically or manufacturer standard details marked up with project dimensions: drains, overflow scuppers, curbs, pipes and clusters, hot stacks, parapet base flashing and termination, wall counterflashing/reglet, expansion joints, equipment supports and dunnage, davit/fall-protection bases, lightning protection bases, roof hatch.
10. Manufacturer's warranty pre-approval letter naming the project, owner, applicator, warranty type (NDL system), term, wind-speed coverage, hail coverage, and any conditions, exclusions, or required inspections.
11. Applicator certification letter from the manufacturer, current and naming the subcontractor's legal entity at the warranty level proposed, plus foreman/crew qualifications if the spec asks.
12. Samples: membrane with a factory seam, color chip if color is selectable, walkway pad, and prefabricated corners/boots; SDS and VOC content for adhesives, primers and sealants with application temperature limits; the manufacturer's installation manual pages for the attachment method and seam welding parameters; pre-roofing requirements including fastener pull-out test procedure and manufacturer rep inspection schedule.

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

13. Membrane polymer, thickness, reinforcement and color in the data sheet against 07 54 00 Part 2 — reject thickness downgrades (60 mil specified, 45 mil submitted) and send any product change back through the substitution request process rather than approving it inside a submittal.
14. Attachment method against the spec and the structural deck: mechanically attached into steel deck is routine; into concrete or gypsum/cementitious wood-fiber deck needs specific fasteners and pull-out tests; fully adhered requires the cover board the manufacturer's listing calls for.
15. Drain and overflow count, location and sump size on the tapered layout against the plumbing roof drain plan and the architectural roof plan; every drain on each must appear on the others.
16. Minimum slope 1/4" per foot (IBC Chapter 15 thermoplastic single-ply section and the spec) on the tapered layout, crickets between drains, and crickets on the upslope side of any curb wider than about 24" — look for flat spots between drains and dead valleys behind curbs.
17. Insulation R-value against the spec and the energy code compliance form on the drawings (COMcheck or ASHRAE 90.1 table); confirm whether the code path uses average R or R at the thinnest point, because tapered systems commonly fall short at the drain sump.
18. Two-layer insulation with staggered joints where specified, cover board joints offset from the top insulation layer, and insulation board size limited (4x4 typical for adhered) per the manufacturer's listing.
19. Uplift calculation inputs against the structural general notes (wind speed, exposure, risk category, topographic factor) and zone widths against the roof dimensions and ASCE 7 edition in the adopted code (ASCE 7-16/22 zone geometry differs from 7-10).
20. Fastener length versus the insulation stack at the thickest point of the taper plus required deck penetration (3/4" through steel deck, manufacturer embedment in concrete or wood), and securement density per zone equal to or greater than the tested assembly.
21. Base flashing height at every wall and curb detail: 8" minimum above finished membrane after taper build-up, counterflashing or termination bar and sealant at the top edge, tie-in to through-wall flashing shown on the architectural wall sections.
22. Drain details against the plumbing drain product: clamping ring and strainer compatible with the membrane, sump insulation thickness, drain extension rings where taper or re-roof raises the surface, and overflow drain dam height above the primary drain inlet.
23. Warranty letter against 07 54 00 Part 1: term (20-year NDL typical for schools), coverage of wind speed at or above the spec's stated mph, hail coverage if specified, and no "conditional" language that excludes conditions actually present on this roof (ponding, rooftop PV, equipment).
24. Curb schedule for mechanical units, skylights, smoke vents and the roof hatch against the taper build-up at each curb location so every curb still stands at least 8" above the finished membrane.
25. Edge metal, coping and gravel stop: confirm whether they are in this submittal or in 07 62 00 / 07 71 00, and that whichever carries them shows ANSI/SPRI/FM 4435 (ES-1) test data for the design pressure and that the membrane manufacturer accepts the edge for the warranty.
26. Cool-roof requirement: aged reflectance/SRI meets IECC C402.3 if the project is in climate zone 1–3, or any LEED / CHPS / district sustainability target, and a non-white membrane selected for appearance has been accepted by the architect and the energy compliance form.
27. Fire-rated roof/ceiling assembly: if the structural or life-safety drawings call a UL P-series design, confirm the submitted insulation, cover board and membrane are permitted in that design.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

28. Steel deck (05 31 00) / concrete (03 30 00): send the fastener data sheet and required deck gauge or pull-out value; get back deck gauge, profile, side-lap and support attachment, and confirmation the deck was designed for the roof assembly dead load — settle before deck shop drawing approval.
29. Plumbing (22 00 00): send the tapered layout with drain and overflow locations and sump depths; get back drain body model, clamping ring, extension rings, overflow dam elevations, and any drain relocation — settle before tapered insulation fabrication release.
30. HVAC (23 00 00): send the roof plan with curb locations and taper thickness at each; get back unit weights, curb sizes, curb heights adjusted for taper, and the refrigerant/condensate/duct penetration list — settle before curbs ship and before deck openings are framed.
31. Roof specialties and sheet metal (07 62 00 / 07 71 00): send the membrane termination and edge details; get back coping, fascia and edge metal shop drawings with ES-1 test reports and the membrane-clad metal product — settle before parapet framing and blocking are complete.
32. Air barrier and sealants (07 27 00 / 07 92 00): send the wall-to-roof transition detail; get back the air barrier material and a compatibility statement (PVC in contact with asphaltic products, TPO with certain sealants) — settle before the wall air barrier reaches the parapet.
33. Electrical and lightning protection (26 00 00 / 26 41 00): send the penetration list and dunnage locations; get back the conduit penetration schedule and lightning base attachment method (adhered bases, no screws through membrane) — settle before membrane installation starts.
34. Metal fabrications and fall protection (05 50 00 / 11 24 00): send locations of equipment supports, ladders, davits and anchor posts; get back base plate sizes, pedestal details and whether each is flashed as a curb or a pipe — settle before support steel fabrication.
35. Skylights and roof accessories (08 60 00 / 07 72 00): send taper thickness at each curb; get back curb dimensions and whether curbs are insulated and self-flashing or require membrane up-and-over — settle before accessory submittal approval.
36. Masonry (04 20 00): send base flashing and termination elevation; get back through-wall flashing and reglet elevation and product — settle before the parapet courses above the roof line are laid.
37. Structural engineer (via RFI): send the assembly dead load, equipment loads and the ponding head at overflow scuppers; get back confirmation of capacity and deflection at low points — settle before this submittal is approved.
38. Future PV / rooftop additions (26 31 00 or owner-furnished): send the roof plan to the manufacturer and designer; get back the accepted attachment or ballast method and walkway layout so the warranty is not voided — settle before warranty issuance.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

39. Warranty pre-approval letter vs system component schedule and data sheets: exact product names, thickness, adhesive, cover board, fasteners, and applicator name.
40. Uplift calculation vs securement drawing: design pressure per zone, zone widths, and fastener/adhesive spacing per zone.
41. Securement drawing vs FM/UL tested assembly listing: deck type and gauge, insulation type and thickness range, cover board, plate size, fastener spacing, membrane sheet width, and adhesive type and rate.
42. Tapered layout vs plumbing drain plan vs architectural roof plan: drain and overflow count, location, sump size, and slope direction.
43. Tapered layout vs energy compliance form: average (or minimum) R-value and insulation thickness the form claims.
44. Tapered layout vs curb schedule and flashing details: taper build-up at each curb vs the 8" base flashing height shown.
45. Flashing details vs architectural wall sections: termination elevation, reglet/counterflashing location, through-wall flashing elevation.
46. Fastener data sheet vs deck shop drawings vs tapered layout: fastener length vs stack-up at the thickest point plus deck penetration; fire classification report vs roof plan: listed maximum slope and deck type vs actual.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

47. Which editions of IBC, IECC and ASCE 7 has the adopting authority (state building code council, or a state school agency such as DSA in California or TDI in Texas) adopted, and is this school Risk Category III or IV (IV if designated a storm shelter or emergency operations facility)? — research; it varies by state and year.
48. Does the adopted IECC/ASHRAE 90.1 path require a minimum continuous insulation R-value above deck, and does the jurisdiction accept average R for tapered systems or require minimum at the drain? — research the adopted edition and local amendments.
49. Is a cool roof (minimum aged SR/SRI) required by climate zone under IECC C402.3, by a local ordinance, or by a district sustainability policy (CHPS, LEED for Schools)? — climate-zone rule from memory; local ordinance and district policy researched.
50. Is Class A roof covering required by IBC 1505 for this construction type (nearly always for schools), and does a Wildland-Urban Interface designation add ignition-resistant requirements? — Class A from memory; WUI mapping researched by locality.
51. Does IPC/UPC require secondary (overflow) roof drainage, how high is the overflow inlet above the primary, and did the EOR design the roof for the resulting ponding head? — code requirement from memory; sizing and head verified with plumbing and structural.
52. For a re-roof, does the IBC reroofing section require tear-off (wet insulation, more than two existing layers) and does the IECC existing-building chapter require bringing insulation to current R-value when the roof assembly is exposed? — IBC rule from memory; IECC trigger researched in the adopted edition.
53. Does the state or jurisdiction require a product approval or evaluation report for the roof assembly and edge metal (Florida Product Approval / Miami-Dade NOA, Texas TDI windstorm, ICC-ES ESR)? — research by state.
54. Does the owner's property insurer or risk pool (FM Global, state school insurance pool) require an FM-approved assembly (1-60/1-90), FM 4473 severe hail class, or FM-approved edge metal beyond what the spec says? — research with the owner.
55. Do VOC rules (SCAQMD, OTC states, CARB) prohibit the solvent-based bonding adhesive and primer submitted, forcing low-VOC or water-based alternates with different temperature limits? — research by jurisdiction.
56. Does the adopted IECC air barrier provision apply at the roof, is the membrane the designated air barrier element, and is a vapor retarder required by climate zone or interior humidity (natatorium, kitchen)? — from memory, verified against the design's air barrier plan.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

57. Seams welded at wrong temperature or speed, or over dusty/contaminated membrane: cold or scorched welds that peel in the first winter; every seam gets probed and re-welded.
58. Ponding at drains and valleys because the tapered layout was built to the drawings while drains were field-relocated, or deck deflection creates low spots: algae, debris, accelerated weathering, and ponding exclusion in the warranty.
59. Fasteners too short for the taper build-up or over-driven through plates: back-out, membrane tenting and puncture, and reduced uplift resistance exactly in corners where the stack is thickest.
60. Mechanically attached membrane fluttering between fastener rows in wind: fatigue at plates and seams, then progressive peel from a perimeter or corner — usually traced to zone widths or spacing taken from the wrong ASCE 7 edition.
61. Wet or dew-covered insulation installed, or boards left uncovered overnight: facer delamination, adhered membrane blisters, trapped moisture, and the manufacturer rep refusing to issue the warranty.
62. Bonding adhesive applied below the temperature limit or without proper flash-off: membrane billows and releases; sheets must be cut and re-adhered.
63. Curbs end up less than 8" above the finished roof because nobody added the taper build-up at that location: water entry at units, curb extensions after equipment is set.
64. Incompatible materials in contact — PVC on asphalt or coal tar, TPO against certain sealants and roofing cements, PVC and TPO welded to each other: plasticizer migration, embrittlement, and cracking at penetrations within a few years.
65. Penetrations added by electrical, mechanical and low-voltage trades after the roof is complete, flashed with mastic or pitch pockets by someone other than the roofer: leaks, and the roofer refuses warranty on the work.
66. Punctures from trades walking and dropping tools, no protection boards or walkway pads during construction: dozens of small leaks found at flood or electronic leak testing, delayed closeout.
67. Drain clamping ring installed with membrane not fully under the ring, missing bolts, or ring over a sealant bead on a cold day: leak straight into the building at the drain.
68. Edge metal installed without a continuous cleat or using untested shop-brake metal: edge peel in a wind event that unzips the whole perimeter.
69. Membrane not relaxed before installation in cold weather, or shrinkage over time pulling base flashing off the parapet: wrinkles, fishmouths and bridged corners that open at the wall.

## 7. Industry standards to verify product claims against

70. ASTM D6878 — Standard Specification for Thermoplastic Polyolefin Based Sheet Roofing (TPO).
71. ASTM D4434 — Standard Specification for Poly(Vinyl Chloride) Sheet Roofing (PVC), and ASTM D6754 — Ketone Ethylene Ester Based Sheet Roofing (KEE).
72. ASTM C1289 — Faced Rigid Cellular Polyisocyanurate Thermal Insulation Board (Type II Class 1 typical), with LTTR per CAN/ULC S770 and PIMA QualityMark certification.
73. ASTM C1177 — Glass Mat Gypsum Substrate, and ASTM C1278 — Fiber-Reinforced Gypsum Panels (cover boards).
74. ASTM E108 / UL 790 — Fire Tests of Roof Coverings (Class A/B/C), and UL 1256 / FM 4450 — Fire tests of roof deck constructions over steel deck.
75. FM 4470 — Approval Standard for Single-Ply, Polymer-Modified Bitumen Sheet, Built-Up Roof and Liquid Applied Roof Assemblies (uplift, hail, foot traffic, fire), with FM Data Sheet 1-28 (wind design) and 1-29 (roof deck securement); UL 580 / UL 1897 as the alternate uplift test.
76. ASCE 7 (edition per adopted code) — Minimum Design Loads, Chapter 30 Components and Cladding roof pressures; ANSI/SPRI WD-1 — Wind Design Standard Practice for Roofing Assemblies.
77. ANSI/SPRI/FM 4435 / ES-1 — Test Standard for Edge Systems Used with Low Slope Roofing Systems; ANSI/SPRI RD-1 — Performance Standard for Roof Drains and Overflow Drainage; ANSI/SPRI FX-1 — Field Test Procedure for Withdrawal Resistance of Roofing Fasteners.
78. CRRC-1 Standard with ASTM C1549 (solar reflectance), ASTM C1371 (emittance) and ASTM E1980 (SRI calculation) for cool-roof claims; FM 4473 — Impact Resistance of Roofing (Class 1–4 hail) for hail claims.
79. ASTM D7877 — Electronic Methods for Detecting and Locating Leaks in Roof Membranes, and ASTM D5957 — Flood Testing Horizontal Waterproofing Installations, for the post-installation acceptance test the spec names; NRCA Roofing Manual: Membrane Roof Systems for detail and tapered-design conventions.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

80. K-12 school (baseline): Risk Category III (IV if a storm shelter) raises wind pressures and may require a sealed uplift calculation; summer-only installation windows push the schedule into the hottest months (adhesive flash-off, membrane expansion) or the shoulder season (cold-weather limits); district standards often dictate 20–30 year NDL, white membrane, walkway pads to every unit, and a state school agency inspector may have to witness pull-out tests and seam probes.
81. Natatorium or commercial kitchen within the school: high interior humidity requires a vapor retarder at the deck, sealed penetrations, and a review of insulation facer and cover board for moisture; condensation complaints become roof "leaks."
82. Hospital or data center: Risk Category IV, often FM-insured requiring FM 1-90 and severe-hail class, redundant drainage, and phased re-roofing with negative-air and odor control that rules out solvent adhesives and hot-air welding near intakes.
83. Warehouse or big-box retail: very large roof areas push mechanically attached wide-sheet systems; review sheet width versus tested assembly, deck gauge versus fastener pull-out, and future PV ballast and roof traffic load.
84. Multifamily or hospitality with rooftop amenity decks: pedestrian traffic requires protected-membrane or pavers on pedestals, a thicker membrane (80 mil) and a different warranty, plus overflow drainage under the deck surface.
85. Coastal or high-wind region on any facility type: corner and perimeter zones can require adhered rather than mechanically attached membranes, FM 1-120 or higher, and state product approvals (Florida, Texas coastal counties) that must be in the submittal before release.
