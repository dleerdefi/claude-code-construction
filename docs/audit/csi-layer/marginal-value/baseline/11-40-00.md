# 11 40 00 Foodservice Equipment — Submittal Review Checklist (GC Project Engineer)

Baseline assumption: K-12 school or hospital kitchen, kitchen equipment contractor (KEC) furnishing and installing, hoods and walk-ins included in the section unless the spec carves them out to 23 38 13 / 13 21 00. Items are numbered continuously across sections.

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Item-numbered equipment schedule keyed to the FS-series drawing item numbers, one row per item including suffixed items (12, 12A) and spare numbers, with manufacturer, model, quantity, and status for each: by KEC, owner-furnished/contractor-installed, owner-furnished/owner-installed, existing-relocated, NIC, future (rough-in only).
2. Manufacturer cut sheet for every scheduled item with the specific model, voltage/phase, options, accessories, casters/legs, and door hinging marked up — a full catalog page with nothing circled is incomplete.
3. Custom stainless fabrication shop drawings (prep tables, sinks, dish tables, pot sinks, serving counters, wall shelves, chef's counters) at scale with plan, elevations, and sections showing alloy (304 vs 430), gauge, finish (#4), top/backsplash/edge profile, sink bowl size and depth, drain basket/lever waste, undershelf, leg and foot type, and field-joint locations.
4. Electrical rough-in drawing (FS-E) listing per item: voltage, phase, amps, kW, connection type (cord-and-plug with NEMA configuration vs direct), disconnect/shunt-trip requirement, rough-in height, and dimensioned location from column lines or finished wall.
5. Plumbing rough-in drawing (FS-P) listing per item: CW/HW size and height, direct vs indirect waste size, floor sink or floor drain served, gas connection size, BTU/hr, required inlet pressure (in. WC), and dimensioned stub-up locations.
6. Mechanical rough-in drawing (FS-M): hood exhaust CFM and static pressure per duct collar, collar size and location, make-up air CFM, dish machine exhaust/condensate hood CFM, and condensing-unit heat rejection for remote refrigeration.
7. Walk-in cooler/freezer package: panel layout with box dimensions and ceiling height, floor type (insulated floor panel vs recessed slab vs floorless cooler), door size/hinging/hardware, heated frames and pressure-relief vents, interior lights, temperature alarm, refrigeration schedule (evaporator and condensing unit model, refrigerant, capacity at design temp, line-set length), condensate drain routing, and condensing-unit mounting location.
8. Exhaust hood package: hood type (I or II), length and overhang, listing data (UL 710 at the listed CFM per lineal foot for the appliance duty category under it), filter type, hood lights, duct collar sizes, fire suppression system (UL 300 listed) with nozzle layout drawn over the actual appliance lineup, manual pull station location, gas valve, and fire alarm interface.
9. Dish machine package: rack/hour, booster heater kW and temperature rise, inlet water temperature it is sized for, rinse temperature, drain type and tempering if required, vent/condensate hood requirement, and chemical feeder interface.
10. Written list of all deviations from the specified basis-of-design on the transmittal, with substitution requests already processed under Division 01 — not discovered line by line inside the package.
11. Seismic anchorage details and calculations (walk-ins, hoods, wall shelving, equipment over the ASCE 7 weight thresholds) stamped by an engineer licensed in the project state where the site is SDC C or higher.
12. Warranty terms per item (1-yr parts and labor standard, 5-yr compressor on reach-ins and ice machines, walk-in panel warranty), startup, demonstration, and training commitments, and wiring diagrams for any field-wired item (conveyor dish machine, remote refrigeration, booster).

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

13. Model-by-model against the spec section's equipment schedule (or the FS drawing schedule if the spec defers to it): any model that is not the specified one or a listed acceptable manufacturer is a substitution, not a submittal.
14. Overall dimensions of each piece against the FS plan and the delivery path: door clear widths, corridor widths, elevator cab size, and floor-to-ceiling along the route for walk-in panels, dish machines, ranges, and combi ovens.
15. Fabricated piece lengths against field conditions — wall-to-wall dimensions, column wraps, filler strips at ends — and that the shop drawings carry a "field verify" note with the dimension the KEC has actually measured or will measure after framing.
16. Each item's electrical load on FS-E against the E-series panel schedules: voltage, phase, breaker size, wire size, receptacle NEMA configuration, and that a submitted model with a different voltage or phase than the basis-of-design has been flagged to the EC.
17. Gas input per item on FS-P against the P-series gas load schedule, meter size, and regulator setting; sum the kitchen load and compare to the branch sizing.
18. Hood exhaust CFM and static on FS-M against the M-series exhaust fan schedule and make-up air unit capacity; confirm the fan is listed for grease duty and the M drawings carry the same hood tag.
19. Indirect-waste items (prep sinks, ice machines, dish machine, walk-in evaporator drains, steamers, kettles) against floor sink locations on the P underslab plan — every indirect drain needs a floor sink within reach of the drain line, with air gap.
20. Slab depressions, floor troughs, floor sink block-outs, and walk-in floor recesses on the FS drawings against the S-series slab plan; if the slab is already poured, verify the as-built depression matches the submitted walk-in floor and dish machine conveyor requirements.
21. Hood height and walk-in box height (plus roof-mounted evaporator/condensing unit and clearance) against ceiling heights and structure above on the A and S drawings.
22. Stainless alloy, gauge, and finish on the fabrication drawings against the spec (typical: 14 ga 304 #4 tops, 16 ga 304 backsplashes and dish tables, 18 ga bodies, 16 ga shelving); check that nothing was quietly submitted as 430.
23. NSF, UL/ETL listing, and ENERGY STAR shown on each cut sheet where the spec or owner standard requires it.
24. Item count: every item number on the FS plan appears in the submittal schedule and vice versa; owner-furnished and existing-relocated items still have rough-ins shown on FS-E/FS-P/FS-M.
25. Wall-mounted items (wall shelves, hand sinks, pot racks, hose reels, mop sink faucets) against blocking/backing on the A-series wall sections and the framing contractor's backing plan.
26. Casters and flexible connectors: gas appliances on casters show a listed flexible connector, quick-disconnect, and restraining cable; electric appliances on casters show cord length adequate to pull the appliance out for cleaning.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

27. Division 26 Electrical: send FS-E rough-in schedule (V/ph/A/kW, NEMA configs, cord lengths, disconnects, shunt-trip breakers for hood suppression, walk-in and refrigeration loads); need back an updated panel schedule and confirmation of circuit sizes and stub locations; settle before in-wall electrical rough-in inspection and before the kitchen slab pour for floor-fed items.
28. Division 22 Plumbing: send FS-P (CW/HW sizes and heights, indirect waste, floor sink locations, gas BTU and pressure, backflow preventers on carbonators/ice machines/dish machines, grease interceptor load); need back the underslab plumbing layout with floor sinks and stub-ups dimensioned and the water heater/booster recovery check for 180°F rinse; settle before underslab plumbing inspection and slab pour.
29. Division 23 HVAC: send FS-M (hood CFM/static, collar sizes and locations, MUA, dish machine exhaust, walk-in condensing unit location and heat rejection); need back the exhaust fan and MUA schedule, grease duct routing with clearances to combustibles, roof curb locations, and refrigeration line-set routing; settle before roof deck/curb layout and before ceiling framing.
30. Fire suppression and fire alarm (Division 21/28): confirm who furnishes and installs the hood UL 300 system (usually the KEC's hood vendor) and who permits it; send nozzle layout and gas valve/shunt-trip requirements; need back fire alarm monitoring point and the fire marshal's permit; settle before hood is hung.
31. Division 03 Concrete: send slab depression dimensions and elevations for walk-in floors, floor troughs, floor sinks, and conveyor dish machine pits; need back confirmation on the slab layout; settle before slab forming.
32. Division 09 Finishes: send equipment footprints and walk-in panel lines; need back quarry tile/epoxy floor extent, cove base behind and under equipment, FRP or tile wall extents, and washable ceiling type in the cooking zone; settle before floor finish starts, because walk-ins and fabricated bases typically sit on finished floor.
33. Division 05/structural: send hood hanging loads with hanger locations, walk-in ceiling loads where condensing units mount on the box, and seismic anchorage reactions; need back hanger support details and roof framing confirmation; settle before deck/roof framing is closed.
34. Division 07 Roofing: send roof penetrations for grease duct, exhaust fan curbs, condensing unit sleepers/curbs, and refrigerant line pitch pockets; need back curb details and flashing; settle before roofing is complete.
35. Division 08 Doors: send delivery path sizes and walk-in door swing; need back door clear openings and confirmation that kitchen corridor doors do not conflict with walk-in or reach-in door swings; settle before door frames are set.
36. Divisions 06/12 millwork and serving line: send KEC serving counter and tray-slide drawings; need back the millwork contractor's scope line (who furnishes the sneeze guard, tray slide, cashier station, front panels) and finish selections; settle before either party releases fabrication.
37. Division 25/BMS and Division 26 controls: send walk-in temperature alarm, demand-control kitchen ventilation sensor, and dish machine interface points; need back BMS point list; settle before controls rough-in.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

38. Spec equipment schedule vs FS plan schedule vs submittal schedule: item number, description, quantity, model, furnish/install status.
39. FS-E rough-in schedule vs E panel schedules: item tag, voltage, phase, amps/kW, breaker size, receptacle type, circuit number.
40. FS-P rough-in schedule vs P fixture connection schedule and underslab plan: connection sizes, indirect vs direct waste, floor sink tag and location, gas size and BTU.
41. FS-M vs M exhaust/MUA schedule: hood tag, exhaust CFM, static pressure, collar count and size, fan tag, MUA CFM.
42. Manufacturer cut sheet vs KEC rough-in drawing: connection point locations, sizes, and heights (the KEC's rough-in template often still reflects the basis-of-design model after a substitution).
43. Hood fire suppression nozzle layout vs the appliance lineup on the FS plan under that hood: each appliance matched to a nozzle type and coverage, and the hood length matched to the lineup plus required overhang.
44. Walk-in panel layout vs A floor plan: box dimensions, door location, wall alignment, and the slab recess on S.
45. Refrigeration schedule vs FS-E and E: condensing unit voltage/phase/MCA/MOP and location vs M/A roof plan.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

46. Has the state or county health department completed plan review on the foodservice layout, and are its comments incorporated in this submittal? (Research: the AHJ, submittal path, and adopted food code edition vary by jurisdiction and the review is often owner- or architect-driven.)
47. Does the hood type (I vs II) and listed CFM match the appliance duty category under it per IMC 507 and NFPA 96, including the 6-inch overhang on open sides? (From memory, verify against the listing.)
48. Is the hood suppression system UL 300 listed and does it shut off gas and electric to the protected appliances on activation per NFPA 96 and NFPA 17A, with fire alarm monitoring if the building has a fire alarm? (From memory; confirm the fire marshal's permit requirement — research locally.)
49. Are hand sinks located so every prep and dish area has one within the distance the adopted food code requires, with no obstruction? (Research the state's adopted FDA Food Code edition and local amendments.)
50. Is the three-compartment sink sized so the largest utensil or pan fits each compartment, and is the dish machine the sanitizing type the food code allows (hot water vs chemical)? (From memory; confirm with health department.)
51. Is the grease interceptor sized per the local plumbing code and the sewer authority's fats/oils/grease ordinance, and are the right fixtures routed through it? (Research: FOG rules are local.)
52. Do food prep sinks, ice machines, dish machines, steamers, and walk-in evaporators discharge through an air gap to a floor sink per the adopted plumbing code (IPC or UPC chapter 8)? (From memory; confirm which code is adopted.)
53. Accessibility: are public-facing serving counters and any employee-accessible elements at the required height and reach, and does the kitchen as an employee work area only need approach/enter/exit under ADA 203.9 or does the state code (e.g., CBC 11B) require more? (From memory; research state amendments.)
54. Seismic: what is the project's SDC and Ip, and does equipment above the ASCE 7 chapter 13 weight thresholds, plus walk-ins, hoods, and shelving, have engineered anchorage? (Research the structural general notes; from memory on the thresholds.)
55. Backflow: are carbonators (ASSE 1022, stainless or non-copper downstream of CO2), dish machines (ASSE 1004 or air gap), and ice machines protected per the adopted plumbing code and water purveyor? (From memory; research local cross-connection program.)
56. Refrigerant: does every self-contained and remote refrigeration unit use a refrigerant that is still legal to install for that equipment class under the current EPA AIM Act technology-transition rules? (Research — the compliance dates have been shifting and the KEC's stock may predate them.)
57. Energy code: does the walk-in meet the DOE/EISA walk-in requirements (strip curtains, door closers, EC evaporator motors, panel R-values) and does the hood system meet IECC/ASHRAE 90.1 limits on exhaust and demand-control ventilation? (From memory; research the adopted energy code edition.)
58. Electrical: are kitchen receptacles GFCI-protected per NEC 210.8(B), is cooking equipment listed to UL 197, and are disconnects within sight of each hard-wired appliance? (From memory.)
59. Gas: are caster-mounted gas appliances connected with listed flexible connectors (ANSI Z21.69), quick-disconnects (ANSI Z21.41), and restraining cables, with an accessible shutoff per NFPA 54/IFGC? (From memory.)
60. Hospital-specific: does the state health department or licensing agency (and in California, HCAi) require a separate foodservice plan review and are walk-ins and critical refrigeration on the essential electrical system per NFPA 99 and the owner's standard? (Research.)

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

61. Floor sinks and stub-ups were poured to the basis-of-design model's connection points; the substituted model's drain is 8 inches off; result is a saw-cut slab, an unsightly trench patch, and a health inspector asking about the open joint.
62. Equipment arrives 208V/3ph and the circuit was run at 480V/3ph (or 208/1ph vs 3ph); result is a buck-boost transformer, a new feeder, or a factory voltage change with a lead time, right before turnover.
63. Walk-in floor recess was not formed or was formed at the wrong depth; result is a ramp into the cooler (ADA and cart problems) or a rebuilt slab section.
64. Hood is the right length for the specified lineup but the owner swapped a griddle for a charbroiler during submittal; the hood is now under-listed for the duty, the nozzles do not cover, and the fire marshal fails the final.
65. Fabricated dish table was built to the plan dimension, not the field dimension; it is 1-1/2 inches long and gets field-cut and re-welded with a visible seam, or short and gaps at the wall that the health inspector wants sealed.
66. Walk-in panels or a rack conveyor dish machine will not make the turn into the kitchen; result is a removed door frame or a cut wall after finishes.
67. Booster heater or water heater recovery sized from the KEC's assumed 110°F inlet while the plumber delivered 120°F or 140°F to a different point, or the booster is 12 kW where 18 kW was needed; rinse never reaches 180°F and the health department conditions the permit.
68. Grease duct routed by the HVAC contractor without clearances to combustibles or with an unlisted wrap; result is reroute above a finished ceiling after the hood is already hung.
69. Remote condensing unit line-set run exceeds the manufacturer's maximum length or lacks oil traps on the vertical rise; the freezer never pulls down and compressor fails under warranty dispute.
70. Owner-furnished items (mixer, slicer, combi oven from a grant purchase) left off the rough-in drawings; result is extension cords or a new circuit after the ceiling is closed.
71. Submittal returned in the fourth month of a six-month kitchen; walk-in panels and custom fabrication at 12-16 weeks lead time become the critical path for the entire finish phase.
72. KEC installs equipment on a tile floor before cove base and epoxy grout are complete; equipment gets moved twice and legs scratch the finished floor.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

81. Hospital: add tray-line/patient tray assembly and cart wash equipment, blast chillers, and nourishment-station ice machines; verify walk-ins and critical reach-ins are on the essential electrical system (NFPA 99) and alarmed to the BMS; FGI Guidelines and the state licensing agency (HCAi in California, with OSHPD-style seismic anchorage and pre-approval, OPM/OSP) add review time and stamped anchorage; infection-control risk assessment governs delivery routes and dust control during install.
82. K-12 school: typically state school-facility agency review on top of health (DSA in California, state education department elsewhere), USDA/NSLP program requirements drive the serving line; serving line and tray-slide heights are lower for elementary (roughly 28-30 inch tray slide) and the sneeze guard is at student eye level; districts often standardize on all-electric kitchens, specific manufacturers, and ENERGY STAR; owner-furnished grant-purchased items are common and must be on the rough-ins; summer-only installation windows compress the schedule.
83. Restaurant: tenant-improvement conditions — landlord criteria for grease duct routing through other tenants, roof curbs, and gas meter capacity; higher-duty cooking (charbroilers, solid fuel) raises hood listing and NFPA 96 solid-fuel requirements; the KEC is often the design-build dealer, so the shop drawings are the only coordinated drawings and the GC must drive the FS-E/FS-P/FS-M reconciliation itself.
84. All types: the health department edition of the FDA Food Code, the FOG ordinance, and the fire marshal's hood permit path are local — identify the AHJ and its plan-review status in the first week of the submittal, not at the inspection.
