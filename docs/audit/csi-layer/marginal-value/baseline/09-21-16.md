# 09 21 16 Gypsum Board Assemblies — Submittal Review Checklist (K-12 baseline, healthcare deltas noted)

## 1. What a complete submittal must show

1. Transmittal maps each item to a 09 21 16 paragraph (board, accessories, fasteners, joint treatment, assemblies, shaft wall) and states whether 09 22 16 framing and 07 84 43 head-of-wall are included or submitted separately.
2. Product data for every board type on the wall-type schedule: regular, Type X, Type C, moisture/mold-resistant, abuse-resistant, impact-resistant, sag-resistant ceiling board, 1" shaft liner, glass-mat sheathing (if carried here) — each with thickness, edge profile, and ASTM C1396 compliance.
3. Fire-rated board shows the UL classification mark and the specific Type X vs Type C designation (Type C is not interchangeable with X in ceiling membranes and most shaft wall designs).
4. Mold/moisture-resistant board shows an ASTM D3273 score of 10; abuse/impact-resistant board shows its ASTM C1629 classification (surface abrasion, indentation, soft-body/hard-body impact levels).
5. Fastener data: Type S (25–20 ga), Type S-12 drill point (18 ga and heavier), Type W for wood blocking; lengths for each layer condition; head style; corrosion coating; ASTM C1002 or C954 compliance.
6. Accessories per ASTM C1047: corner bead, L/J bead, casing bead, control joints (zinc or vinyl, e.g., 093-type), reveal/architectural trims — material called out for each location (vinyl/zinc in wet or exterior-exposed locations).
7. Joint treatment per ASTM C475: tape type (paper vs fiberglass mesh), compound types (taping, topping, all-purpose, setting-type), and the Level 5 skim/primer-surfacer product when Level 5 is specified.
8. Acoustical sealant (ASTM C919 application), putty pads, and resilient channel or isolation clips for every STC-rated wall type.
9. Shaft wall system as a system: C-H or C-T stud, J-runner, 1" liner, with the manufacturer's limiting-height table at the specified deflection (L/240 or L/120) and lateral pressure (5 psf interior; 10 psf intermittent at elevator hoistways), plus the UL design (e.g., U415/U428/U438) it rides on.
10. A copy of every UL (or GA-600/ICC-ES) fire-rated design referenced on the wall-type legend, with the submitted products highlighted against each component line of the design.
11. STC test reports (ASTM E90/E413) for each rated partition, from a lab test of the same stud depth/gauge/spacing, layer count, insulation, and resilient channel — not a "calculated" or "estimated" value.
12. Shop drawings where the spec requires them: shaft walls, curved walls, soffits/bulkheads, gypsum ceilings, area-separation walls — showing framing size/gauge/spacing, bracing, deflection track, board orientation/joint staggering, and control-joint locations.
13. Sustainability/VOC data if specified: SCAQMD 1168 compliance for sealant/compound, EPD/HPD for board, recycled content; domestic-steel certification if the funding source requires it.
14. Manufacturer installation instructions for shaft wall and for abuse-resistant board (both carry fastener-spacing and edge-distance rules that differ from C840 defaults).

## 2. Conformance checks against specs and drawings

15. Build a matrix from the wall-type sheet (stud depth/gauge/spacing, layers each side, board type, insulation, rating, STC, UL design) and tick every row against the submittal; any wall type missing from the submittal is a reject.
16. Overlay the wall-type plan on the Life Safety plan: every corridor, shaft, stair, incidental-use, and occupancy-separation line must land on a rated wall-type variant, not the non-rated twin.
17. Where a UL design lists Type C (horizontal membranes, 1-hr floor-ceiling, most shaft wall face layers), confirm Type C was submitted — not Type X.
18. Shaft wall: read actual shaft heights off building sections and compare to the limiting-height table at the specified gauge and deflection; confirm the listing covers fire exposure from the side(s) the design requires (many shaft wall designs are rated from the finished side only — check the listing text for both-side exposure).
19. Fastener length per layer: minimum 3/8" penetration into steel beyond the last layer; two layers of 5/8" need 1-5/8" min second-layer screws; confirm drill-point screws wherever board attaches to 16-ga or heavier framing (exterior CFMF under 05 40 00).
20. Abuse/impact-resistant board locations: pull the room list from the finish schedule and wall-type notes (corridors, gym, locker rooms, cafeteria, stairs, SPED rooms) and confirm the submitted C1629 level matches each.
21. Moisture-resistant board at toilet, kitchen, janitor, and locker walls; confirm no gypsum (even MR) is proposed behind tile in showers/wet areas where the spec or IBC 2509 requires cement/glass-mat backer under 09 28 00.
22. Ceilings/soffits on the RCP: confirm board thickness vs framing spacing (5/8" at 24" o.c.; 1/2" only if sag-resistant and permitted), and that ceiling board under a rated floor-ceiling membrane is the type the UL design names.
23. Control joints: spec maximum spacing (typically 30 ft walls, 50 ft ceilings with perimeter relief, 30 ft without) vs elevations/shop drawings; any control joint in a rated wall needs a listed rated control-joint detail.
24. STC walls: compare test-report stud spacing and insulation to the drawings (a 24" o.c. test used to justify a 16" o.c. wall, or thinner insulation, is not conforming); confirm resilient channel orientation and sealant called out.
25. Level of finish: compare the GA-214 level schedule against the 09 91 23 sheen schedule and lighting conditions (gym side-lit walls, corridors with clerestory) — Level 5 wherever eggshell or higher sheen meets critical light.
26. Substitutions: if the submittal uses a different manufacturer than the basis of design, confirm the product is a listed component of the same UL design number (several 1-hr ceiling and shaft designs are manufacturer-specific).
27. Deflection track: submitted product (slotted, double-track, or proprietary) must appear in the HW-D listing and provide the gap the structural notes require; board must be shown held below the track flange.
28. Trim profiles: compare architectural reveal/float details (admin lobby, media center) to the exact profile submitted; confirm compatible compound and tape.

## 3. Coordination with other trades

29. 09 22 16 Non-Structural Metal Framing — send the wall-type matrix and shaft heights; need back stud gauge/depth/spacing, bridging, and head-of-wall detail; settle before top-track layout on each level.
30. 07 84 43 Joint Firestopping / 07 84 13 Penetration Firestopping — send rated wall-type list, UL wall designs, and deck conditions (fluted vs flat, beam intersections); need back HW-D/BW-S/WW-D systems referencing those exact wall designs; settle before top track is set.
31. 07 21 00 Thermal/Acoustical Insulation — send the STC test reports; need back confirmation that submitted batt thickness/density matches; settle before insulation submittal approval.
32. 26 05 33 Electrical Boxes and 27/28 low-voltage — send rated/STC wall types; need back a box plan with no back-to-back boxes in the same cavity, 24" horizontal separation or putty pads per UL; settle before rough-in.
33. 23 00 00 HVAC and 23 33 13 Dampers — send shaft wall layout and rated-wall plan; need back damper sleeve sizes and locations so framed openings and sleeves match listings; settle before shaft wall framing.
34. 08 11 13 Hollow Metal Frames — send computed wall thicknesses (stud + layers each side) and rating per opening; need back frame throat dimensions and labels; settle before frame release (long lead, 8–12 weeks).
35. 06 10 00 Blocking / 10 28 00 Toilet Accessories / 10 11 00 Markerboards / 11 66 00 Gym Equipment / 10 51 00 Lockers / 12 30 00 Casework — send a backing plan request; need back mounting heights and loads; settle before board close-in on each area.
36. 14 24 00 Elevators — send shaft wall system data and hoistway clear dimensions; need back rail-bracket points (should attach to structure, not shaft wall), divider beams, and sill support; settle before shaft wall erection, which precedes rail install.
37. 22 Plumbing — send wet-wall board types; need back carrier depths and access-panel locations (rated panels in rated walls); settle before framing.
38. 09 91 23 Painting — send level-of-finish map; need back primer/sheen confirmation and mock-up location; settle before tape-and-finish starts.
39. 09 51 13 ACT / 21 13 00 Sprinkler — send gypsum ceiling/soffit limits from RCP; need back head locations in gypsum areas; settle before soffit framing.

## 4. Documents that must agree with each other

40. Wall-type legend UL design number = UL design in this submittal = wall design referenced in the HW-D firestop submittal = rating shown on the Life Safety plan.
41. UL design component list (board type/thickness, layer count, fastener type/length/spacing) = board data sheet = fastener data sheet.
42. STC test report (stud spacing, insulation, channel) = 09 22 16 framing submittal = 07 21 00 insulation submittal = wall-type schedule.
43. Shaft wall limiting-height table (stud depth/gauge, deflection, pressure) = shaft heights on building sections = hoistway dimensions on elevator shop drawings.
44. Deflection-track product = HW-D listing = structural deflection criterion in general notes.
45. Hollow metal frame throat dimension (08 11 13 schedule) = overall wall thickness derived from the submitted stud depth and board layers.
46. Control-joint spacing/locations: spec text = elevations = shop drawings = rated control-joint detail where in a rated wall.
47. Level-of-finish map = finish schedule = painter's submittal assumptions.
48. Abuse/impact board room list: finish schedule = wall-type notes = product and C1629 level submitted.

## 5. Code / regulatory questions to validate

49. Does each rated wall type meet the hourly rating the adopted IBC assigns (corridors Table 1020, shafts 713.4 by stories connected, stairs 1023, incidental uses 509)? — Life Safety plan first, then adopted IBC edition; answer from memory, confirm edition/amendments.
50. For non-symmetric assemblies (shaft walls, walls with different layers each side), is the listing tested from both sides or only one, and does the AHJ (building official) accept the one-sided rating for this exposure (IBC 703)? — research the UL listing text.
51. Are head-of-wall and wall-to-wall joint systems listed to UL 2079 with this specific wall design and deck type (IBC 715)? — answer from memory, verify design numbers.
52. Does the building's Risk Category III / Seismic Design Category require partition and shaft wall bracing per ASCE 7 Chapter 13, and did the structural EOR delegate it? — research with the EOR; in California, DSA review applies.
53. Does the state school facilities authority impose standards beyond IBC (e.g., abuse resistance, acoustical minimums per ANSI/ASA S12.60 for classrooms, DSA/TEA/NJ SDA review)? — research the funding/approval agency.
54. Where recessed fixtures penetrate a rated ceiling membrane, does the UL design permit them and require ceiling radiation dampers? — research the UL design.
55. Does the hoistway side of the shaft wall create projections/recesses that require bevels under ASME A17.1 2.1.6? — research with elevator consultant/inspector.
56. Does the public funding source trigger Buy American or state domestic-steel rules for studs and accessories? — research the contract's funding conditions.
57. Are the mold-resistant and abuse-resistant requirements code-driven or owner-standard only, and does the owner's design standard override the spec where they conflict? — answer from the owner's standards document.

## 6. Typical failure modes seen in the field

58. Type X delivered where Type C was listed (ceiling membrane, shaft wall face); caught at rated-wall inspection; tear-out of finished ceiling.
59. Second-layer screws too short (1-1/4" into two layers of 5/8"); inspector pull-tests; re-fasten whole wall, re-finish.
60. Shaft wall erected beyond its limiting height or with lighter gauge than listed; wall bows, hoistway out of plumb, rail brackets won't fit; rebuild from inside the shaft.
61. Board run tight to deck or screwed through deflection track slots; head-of-wall cracks after live load, HW-D voided; cut back and re-firestop.
62. Back-to-back electrical boxes in STC-50 classroom walls, no putty pads, no sealant at bottom track; field STC in the 30s; corridor noise complaints after occupancy.
63. Control joints omitted along 100-ft corridors; cracking through the first winter; cosmetic repairs through every school break.
64. Board stored on slab under an unenclosed roof; mold on paper face; owner rejects and the entire stock is replaced.
65. Regular board swapped for abuse-resistant in gym/locker rooms because of availability; discovered at first damage; replace walls in an occupied building.
66. Shaft liner cut short at the top J-runner or horizontal joints not staggered; gap in the rated membrane; fails the fire inspector's shaft walk-down.
67. Level 4 instead of Level 5 on side-lit gym and corridor walls with eggshell; joints photograph; skim and repaint after punch.
68. Galvanized steel corner bead at exterior-wall interior face or wet rooms; rust bleed within two years.
69. Tile installed on moisture-resistant gypsum in showers; board softens; demo and re-tile.
70. Rated-wall and above-ceiling inspection missed before close-in; AHJ requires board removal at random locations.

## 8. Facility-type specifics that change the review

82. K-12: abuse- and impact-resistant board is the norm in corridors, gyms, locker rooms, cafeteria, and stair enclosures; STC 50 classroom-to-corridor and 55 to mechanical/music rooms per S12.60; expect Level 5 in gyms and side-lit corridors; blocking plan dominated by markerboards, casework, and gym equipment.
83. K-12: Risk Category III and state school authority review add seismic bracing of partitions/shaft walls and often a domestic-steel requirement; owner district standards may mandate specific board brands or MR board throughout.
84. Healthcare: FGI Guidelines govern STC by adjacency (patient rooms, exam, consult) and smoke-compartment walls become a second rated-wall family to check against the Life Safety plan; HCAI/OSHPD (CA) or state health department reviews assemblies and may require pre-approved listings.
85. Healthcare: lead-lined gypsum board at imaging rooms (coordinate with physicist's shielding report, 13 49 00) and RF/MRI shielding interfaces; mold-resistant board throughout; Level 5 with epoxy/scrubbable finishes in procedure and clean areas; backing plan driven by handrails, bumper rails, headwalls, and ceiling-mounted equipment.
86. Healthcare: ICRA infection-control barriers (temporary rated/sealed partitions) are a submittal item of their own, and wall penetrations/sealing requirements for pressurized rooms (negative/positive) change the acoustical-sealant and firestop scope at every perimeter.
