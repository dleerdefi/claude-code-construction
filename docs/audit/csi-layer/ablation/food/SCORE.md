## (c) Judgment

A PE would rather receive review B, by a small margin, because it delivers the same thirteen catches, the same correct disposition and the same escalation of the slab and hood items in a document where roughly a quarter of the ledger rows are filler rather than two-thirds, so a reader verifying coverage sees evidence (page, value, governing clause) on almost every row instead of 148 "na" one-liners. Both reviews handle the regulatory traps exactly as the KEY demands: the tray-slide height, the hood/fire-marshal question and the health-department resubmission are all left open with the right authorities named and nothing answered from memory, and neither review takes any of the seven bait items. A's genuine advantages are the consolidated pre-pour hold list (F-25) and a few extra minor catches (undimensioned FS-E exit, unstruck catalog pages, missing QD hose kits), which a PE would use; its liability is F-07, a frost-heave question with no basis in the documents, rated "high" and placed on the hold list, which could delay a pour over something the reviewer invented. B's extras are smaller (FS-19 lighting load, per-item listings) and its F-07 is marginal, but nothing in B risks a wrong field decision. On the three items the caller singled out, the two are effectively tied on substance; B's two-finding treatment of the tray slide and its separate masonry routing row read slightly more precisely, while A's backing routing is slightly richer, so the decision comes down to signal-to-noise, where B is clearly better.
